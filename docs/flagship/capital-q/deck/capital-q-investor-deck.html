QInvestment memorandum · §01 Problem
§01 — Problem

Founders cannot see how investable they are. Investors cannot see enough to decide quickly.

founders

  • Do not know which evidence investors weigh, or what is missing
  • Repeat the same story to every investor in a different format
  • Get silence instead of a reason

investors

  • Screen inbound by hand, in inboxes, forms and spreadsheets
  • Mix self-reported claims with verified facts
  • Lose the relationship history between tools

The public line: “Discovering the right investment opportunity shouldn’t take months. It should take minutes.” Self-reported · FDN page

QInvestment memorandum · §02 Solution
§02 — Solution

One institutional analyst — Q — across preparation, discovery, intake, diligence and the relationship.

One Q
Users experience one analyst. Specialists work inside it; none is user-selectable, and none talks peer-to-peer.
One record
One canonical company, one investor organisation, one company–investor relationship that both sides share.
Human say
Q has intelligence authority; people keep commercial authority. Prepare → Recommend → Approve → Execute.

What Capital Q is not: a marketplace, a CRM, a chatbot, a document store or a fundraising directory. Relationship state is computed from an append-only event history by a deterministic projector — never by a model.

evidence
  • “One institutional investment analyst throughout” — Final System Review §2 Self-reported · owner product source
  • UNIQUE (company, investor organisation) relationship; GateQ and Discover resolve to the same row — research §8 deployed
  • Consequential actions need human approval bound to the exact payload — repository architecture Self-reported

§04 — Assessment

Scores appear only when the shared evidence supports them. Unknown is never zero.

ScoreHow it works
Investability
0–100		FDN’s InvestIQ methodology, nine dimensions; InvestIQ overall × 25. Investor-eligible evidence only, identical for every investor. Below coverage gates it reads “not enough shared evidence yet”.
Suitability
out of 10		Fit to this investor’s mandate. Never blended with Investability on display. Ranking after hard eligibility uses fit-order.v2: Suitability 0.75, Investability 0.25.
Unicorn Potential
0–100, premium		Deterministic indicator of present evidence against a 12-pillar, 24-criterion method. “Not a probability or validated forecast.” Production: 0 of 461 companies have a numeric score — evidence coverage is below the 60% gate.

QInvestment memorandum · §05 Trust
§05 — Trust

A two-sided product only works if neither side can be used against the other.

  • Context Firewall. Founder-private information never silently alters investor-facing ranking, discoverability or assessment. Release-blocking, enforced at retrieval, features and the database.
  • No pay-to-rank, ever. Payment never influences ranking.
  • Deterministic, explainable ranking. Versioned configuration; no model in the feed’s ranking path; never optimised for watch time.
  • Three separate evidence axes. Truth class, evidence status and lifecycle are kept apart; contradictions coexist until reconciled.
  • EU data residency. Services in Railway europe-west4; database in Supabase eu-central-1.
evidence
  • Founder-private data invariant — Final System Review Finding 2 Self-reported · owner product source
  • No pay-to-rank — ADR 0034 and root CLAUDE.md Owner-provided
  • Hosting and residency — STATE.md, ADR 0014 Self-reported

QInvestment memorandum · §11 Defensibility
§11 — Defensibility

The moat is trust that compounds: evidence, relationships and a method investors can audit.

  • Evidence graph. Each confirmed claim moves a company from self-reported toward document-supported and verified, and stays with its provenance.
  • Shared relationship history. Both sides work on one append-only record — hard to replicate with a CRM on one side and a form on the other.
  • A named method. InvestIQ (FDN) and versioned scoring that refuses to score on thin evidence.
  • Neutrality as policy. No pay-to-rank is a commitment competitors that sell placement cannot copy.

Assumption These compound only with enough evidence-rich companies and active investors; today most evidence is self-reported, so most scores stay hidden.

evidence
  • Differentiation claims — locked product sources Self-reported
  • Compounding argument — analyst reading Assumption
  • Calibrated scores need an FDN pilot cohort Assumption
