|                       | Lean launch                                  | Normal                   | Comfortable                  |
| --------------------- | -------------------------------------------- | ------------------------ | ---------------------------- |
| Who it's for          | Today's traffic, a few hundred users a month | About 1,000 people a day | About 10,000 people a day    |

Most of the running cost is AI usage. It only goes up when more people use the product, and the prices below are set so every paying user covers their own usage with a profit. The fixed part (servers, database, email, a company email account for Q's meeting assistant) is small: under $3,000 a month even at the top level.

There are daily spending limits built in, so a bad day can't empty the account. We raise them as income grows.

## 3. Taking payments

We'll use **Paystack** for Nigeria and Africa, and **Stripe** for international customers. Both check the business before they pay out money (KYC). For that we'll need:

- **Paystack:** the company's CAC registration documents, the tax ID (TIN), ID for each director and anyone owning 25% or more, and a company bank account in exactly the same name as the CAC certificate.
- **Stripe:** a company registered in a country Stripe supports, such as the US or UK. Nigeria isn't one, which is why the US company above is needed for foreign card payments.

Capital Q never holds or moves anyone's investment money. It only records commitments, so this is just for subscriptions.

## 4. Marketing

We need to tell people we exist:

- Company pages on LinkedIn, X and Instagram (free).
- Launch content: a short demo video, founder and investor stories, posts in startup and investor communities (free to low cost).
- Paid ads, mainly on LinkedIn, to reach investors and founders. A click there costs around $9, so $300 a month buys roughly 30–35 visits. That's enough to test the message before spending more.
