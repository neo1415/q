## How anonymisation works

- **The tombstone.** `identity.user_profiles` is referenced by 206 foreign keys. On deletion, the profile row stays and is closed:
  - its name becomes "Former user";
  - its given name, family name, headline, photo key, locale, time zone and country are cleared;
  - `closed_at` is set.

  The sign-in account (`auth.users`) is then removed through the Auth admin API, which ends every session. The foreign key now sets null. A check lets only a closed profile lose its auth account. Every kept row therefore still points at a valid id, and that id no longer identifies a person. This anonymises append-only history (audit, relationship events, messages, deal records) without rewriting it.

- **Text that names the person** in another party's record is replaced by statements listed per table (`scrub`). Examples are meeting participants, transcript speaker names, GateQ application contact details, invitation addresses and email addresses in relationship mail.
- **Erasure mode.** Five append-only guards hold a person's name or private words in text:
  - meeting events;
  - the meeting roster;
  - meeting host notes;
  - private observations;
  - inbound email.

  Two revision guards cover claims and knowledge. These guards allow the narrow erasure change only inside the deletion job's transaction (`SET LOCAL cq.data_rights_erasure = on`). Even then, what was said in a meeting cannot be rewritten (pgTAP `999_data_rights`).

- **Q memory and knowledge.** Memory goes through its own lifecycle: status `forgotten`, `valid_to` set, words replaced by the erased marker. Claims and knowledge objects about the person get a new SUPERSEDED revision carrying the marker (claims are never deleted). Earlier revisions' statements are replaced by the marker under erasure mode. Nothing bypasses the Write Gate's history.
- **Rows another record depends on.** When some of a person's own rows are still referenced by kept records (for example, a Q conversation referenced by a run record), the job does two things:
  - it deletes the rest one by one;
  - it keeps the referenced rows with their content emptied (`scrub`), counted as `keptReferenced` in the deletion report.
- **Organisations.**
  - **Only member:** the organisation closes with the account (status `closed`), after the person confirms it by name.
  - **Only owner, with other members:** deletion is refused until they make someone else an owner (Team settings).
  - **Anything else:** their membership ends.

  The rule is checked again when the job runs. Documents the person uploaded to an organisation that continues stay with it. Those of an organisation that closes are removed from storage.

- **Storage objects removed:** the person's export ZIPs, their profile pictures (the upload and the processed image), and the documents of organisations that close with the account.
- **The record of the deletion itself.** The `identity.account_deletion_requests` row (profile id and counts only) and an `audit.security_events` row `account_deleted` are kept.

## What the export contains

There is one JSON file per table marked "Export" below, holding only the rows the predicate names as the person's own. Uploaded files are included too: clean document versions and profile pictures. These are never exported:

- columns that look like credentials (tokens, ciphertext, hashes, code verifiers, embeddings, device keys);
- rows that are only _about_ the person in another party's private record, such as an investor's notes or Q's research on counterparties.

Limits: 20,000 rows per table, 200 files and 400 MiB of files. Anything beyond them is listed in `manifest.json`.

## Open legal questions (for counsel)

1. **Retention periods.** The audit and security records (2 years), billing and tax records ("typically 6 years") and usage analytics (13 months) follow the privacy policy draft. Counsel needs to confirm:
   - Nigeria's statutory periods (tax, company law, the NDPC GAID 2025);
   - the EU and UK periods.
2. **Identity and business verification evidence.** `core.identity_submissions` and `core.kyb_submissions` are retained with no period set. Counsel needs to confirm whether AML or KYC rules apply, and for how long.
3. **Pseudonymised history.** Kept rows still carry the closed profile's opaque id, and messages and meeting words the person sent stay in the recipient's records (privacy policy section 10). Counsel needs to confirm that this meets the GDPR and NDPA standard for these records, or whether message bodies must also be removed on request.
4. **Meeting records owned by the organiser.** The person's own meeting notes (`communication.meeting_assistants`) are deleted. The shared transcript events stay with names removed. Counsel needs to confirm this split, and the consent position for all-party-consent jurisdictions (see decision 108).
5. **Backups.** Deleted data remains in backups until the rolling cycle overwrites it. The owner needs to confirm the backup cycle so the policy can state it.
6. **Provider-held copies.** Not done by this job, and listed so they can be handled:
   - revoking the Google token at Google (the stored token is destroyed);
   - deleting pitch videos held by the video provider;
   - payment-processor customer records.
