Version: 1.0 (Draft)
Status: Active
Document Owner: Product Architecture
Classification: Internal
1. Purpose
The Capital Q Product Architecture Decision Log (PADL) is the authoritative record of the foundational architectural decisions governing the design, development, and evolution of Capital Q.
Its purpose is to document what has been decided, why those decisions were made, and how they should guide future product development.
The PADL is intended to ensure architectural consistency across product, design, engineering, AI systems, and future leadership.
It is not a Product Specification.
The PADL records decisions.
The Product Specification explains implementation.
2. Relationship to Other Product Documents
The Capital Q documentation framework consists of six layers:
1. Vision
Why Capital Q exists.
↓
2. Product Architecture Decision Log (PADL)
What has been architecturally decided.
↓
3. Product Specifications
How each product area works.
↓
4. Technical Architecture
How the system is engineered.
↓
5. Engineering Specifications
Implementation-ready technical requirements.
↓
6. Source Code
The final implementation.
No implementation should contradict a locked PADL decision.
3. Decision Lifecycle
Every architectural decision follows one of three states:
Proposed
The concept is under discussion.
Draft
A preferred direction exists but remains open to refinement.
🔒 Locked
The decision has been approved and becomes part of Capital Q's official architecture.
Only Locked decisions are considered authoritative.

Assessment Always Precedes Advice
Decision
Capital Q separates business assessment from business coaching.
During the assessment phase, Q's sole responsibility is to understand the business and build accurate investment intelligence.
Recommendations, coaching, debriefing, and improvement planning occur only after the assessment has been completed.
Rationale

Assessment Mode
Responsible for:
Reviewing uploaded documents.
Conducting the Founder Intelligence Interview.
Collecting evidence.
Investigating inconsistencies.
Building the Investment Intelligence Record.

Debrief Mode
Responsible for:
Explaining Business Scores.
Explaining Intelligence Confidence.
Identifying weaknesses.
Prioritising improvements.
Recommending FDN Advisory services where appropriate.
Introducing relevant ecosystem partners where specialist support is required.
Every recommendation must be directly supported by evidence gathered during Assessment Mode.

Decision #017
Every Assessment Produces Two Independent Scores
Decision
Every Investment Intelligence dimension shall generate two independent measurements.
Business Score
The Business Score measures the quality and strength of the business within a particular assessment dimension.
It answers:
"How strong is this aspect of the business?"
Intelligence Confidence
The Intelligence Confidence score measures Capital Q's confidence in its own assessment based on the quality, completeness, reliability, and recency of the available evidence.
It answers:
"How confident are we that this assessment is accurate?"
A low Intelligence Confidence score does not necessarily indicate a weak business.
It indicates that additional evidence is required before Capital Q can produce a high-confidence assessment.
Rationale
Traditional scoring systems communicate conclusions.
Capital Q communicates both conclusions and confidence.
This distinction improves transparency while helping investors distinguish between business weakness and insufficient information.
Implications
Every Business Score displayed within Capital Q should be accompanied by its corresponding Intelligence Confidence score.
The platform should always distinguish between poor performance and insufficient evidence.

Decision

The platform shall support four complementary discovery modes:
1. Search
Investor-led, intentional discovery.

Algorithmically personalised discovery.

Implications
Each discovery mechanism should solve a different investor need and should not be optimised using identical algorithms or success metrics.

Chapter 4 – InvestIQ Assessment Engine
Chapter Purpose
This chapter defines the institutional assessment methodology that powers InvestIQ, the proprietary intelligence engine at the core of Capital Q. It establishes how businesses are evaluated, how evidence is interpreted, how institutional intelligence is generated, and how assessments are communicated to founders and investors. The purpose of InvestIQ is not to replace investor judgement or professional due diligence, but to reduce uncertainty, improve transparency, and enable more informed capital decisions through an explainable, evidence-based framework.
Architectural Philosophy
InvestIQ is an institutional reasoning engine, not simply a scoring system. Every assessment should be objective, explainable, evidence-based, context-aware, and proportional to a company's stage of development. The methodology prioritises institutional trust over false precision, transparency over black-box outputs, and actionable intelligence over static reports. Throughout the framework, InvestIQ exists to augment professional judgement rather than replace it.
Decision #60 – InvestIQ Shall Use a Multi-Dimensional Institutional Assessment Framework
Decision

Decision #72 – InvestIQ Shall Maintain One Institutional Truth Across All Users
Decision
InvestIQ shall generate a single objective institutional assessment for every business. Regardless of whether the user is a founder, investor, advisor, or internal administrator, the underlying assessment, evidence, and institutional conclusions shall remain identical.
However, while the underlying intelligence remains constant, Q shall adapt the presentation, explanations, recommendations, and available actions according to the user's role, objectives, and permissions.
This principle establishes a clear separation between:
Institutional Intelligence (which remains constant).
User Experience (which adapts to the individual user).
Capital Q shall therefore maintain one institutional truth while delivering multiple contextual experiences.
Rationale
This decision emerged from one of the most important architectural discussions in Chapter 4.
Initially, we explored whether founders and investors should receive different assessments.
For example:
Should founders receive more optimistic interpretations?
Should investors receive more conservative analyses?
Should different users see different scores?
After considerable discussion, we concluded that this would fundamentally undermine trust.
If founders discovered that investors were seeing a different assessment from the one they received, confidence in the platform would immediately erode.
Likewise, investors need confidence that the intelligence presented to them has not been altered depending on the audience.
The institutional assessment itself must therefore remain objective and universal.
However, we also recognised that founders and investors have fundamentally different objectives.
For example:
An investor opening a company profile wants answers such as:
Should I explore this opportunity further?
What are the major investment considerations?
Which documents should I review next?
Should I schedule a meeting?
A founder opening the exact same assessment wants answers such as:
Why did I receive this score?
What should I improve?
Which weaknesses matter most?
How do I become more investment-ready?
The underlying intelligence is identical.
The guidance is different.
This led to one of the core architectural principles of Capital Q:
One Institutional Truth. Different Contextual Experiences.
Another discussion reinforced this principle when designing the assessment outputs.
We agreed that Layers 1 through 4 of the assessment would remain identical for both founders and investors, while Layer 5 would adapt according to the user's role.
For example:
Founder
Recommended Next Steps:
Strengthen governance.
Improve investor materials.
Complete the data room.
Engage FDN Advisory.
Work with recommended partners.
Investor
Suggested Actions:
Schedule a meeting.
Request additional documentation.
Compare with similar companies.
Open the Due Diligence Workspace.
Save to watchlist.
Notice that neither user sees a different assessment.
Only their next actions differ.
This architecture preserves both institutional integrity and an excellent user experience.
Implications
Every business has a single objective institutional assessment regardless of who views it.
Founders and investors develop greater trust in Capital Q because they know the platform does not manipulate assessments for different audiences.
Q can personalise explanations, workflows, recommendations, and actions without compromising assessment integrity.
Product design becomes significantly cleaner because assessment logic remains centralised while UX adapts independently.
Future user roles (accelerators, corporates, family offices, development finance institutions, advisors, etc.) can receive customised experiences without requiring separate assessment methodologies.
This principle becomes one of the foundational architectural decisions underpinning the entire Capital Q platform.

Internal operating procedures.
Process maturity.
Operational infrastructure.
Business continuity.
Internal systems.
Organisational workflows.
Operational controls.

Additional documentation?
Operational audits?
Management observations?

Decision #74 – Capital Strategy Shall Assess Fundraising Clarity Rather Than Prescribe Capital Decisions

Capital Planning – Has the company clearly articulated its use of funds, expected runway, deployment strategy, and anticipated outcomes?
Investor Alignment – Which investors naturally align with the company's stage, geography, sector, thesis, ticket size, and fundraising profile?

Certain investors were inappropriate.

"You should not be raising venture capital."

"This business should pursue debt instead."

Why the company is raising.
Why it is raising now.
Why it has selected this amount.
How the capital will be deployed.
What milestones this funding round is expected to achieve.

Capital Q simply makes it immediately accessible.

Rather than deciding whether a company has chosen the correct investors, InvestIQ should determine which investors naturally align with the company's profile based on objective characteristics already captured within the platform.
This supports Capital Q's marketplace philosophy without interfering with investor autonomy or founder choice.
Finally, this decision reinforces one of the platform's most important commercial distinctions.
Capital Q provides intelligence, preparation, transparency, and matching.
FDN Advisory provides strategic fundraising advice.
Maintaining this separation protects both products while strengthening their complementary relationship.

Capital Q preserves investor autonomy by facilitating informed decisions rather than recommending investment outcomes.

Identifying missing documentation.
Improving collaboration between founders and investors.

Helping investors conduct due diligence
and
Conducting due diligence for investors.

"Due diligence completed."

"Based on the documents currently available..."

Accelerate workflows.

Investment committees.

Accountants.
Auditors.
Corporate finance advisors.
Professional judgement.

Decision #76 – InvestIQ Shall Evolve Through Governed, Version-Controlled Methodology Rather Than Autonomous Learning

Document summarisation.
Information extraction.
Conversational intelligence.

Matching.
Workflow automation.
User experience.

InvestIQ methodology version.
Assessment timestamp.

Assessment assumptions where applicable.

"How does InvestIQ stay relevant five or ten years from now?"
Markets evolve.
Investor expectations change.
Regulations change.
Industries emerge.
Artificial intelligence improves.
A static methodology would eventually become outdated.

Institutional investors require consistency.
If the methodology changes automatically without transparency, users lose confidence in both historical assessments and future conclusions.

Imagine a company receives an Institutional Assessment of 84 today.

The business changed.
The evidence changed.

Product Intelligence

Better document understanding.
Better natural language conversations.
Better search.
Better matching.
Better recommendations.

New investment practices emerge.
Regulations evolve.
New sectors require specialised assessment modules.
Internal methodology reviews identify improvements.
Institutional governance approves revisions.

If an investor asks:
"Why did this company receive this assessment eighteen months ago?"

"InvestIQ Version 2.1 is now available."

Schedule a meeting.
Request additional documentation.
Compare similar businesses.

First:
"Is this worth my attention?"

"Show me the evidence."

Finally, this architecture supports long-term scalability.
Future assessment modules, AI insights, sector intelligence, and enterprise capabilities can be introduced as additional layers without redesigning the overall user experience.
Implications

Chapter 5 – Decision #80
Decision
Q Shall Function as a Hybrid Intelligence System That Dynamically Adapts Its Behaviour Based on User Intent, Context, and Decision Complexity
Rationale
One of the earliest architectural questions addressed during the design of Q concerned how it should behave during user interactions.
Rather than viewing Q as having a single conversational style, the discussion recognised that users interact with Capital Q for fundamentally different reasons.
Sometimes users simply want Q to perform quick operational tasks, such as locating documents, opening pages, summarising information, or answering straightforward factual questions.
At other times, users require sophisticated strategic thinking involving fundraising decisions, investor selection, capital strategy, governance improvements, valuation considerations, or investment analysis.
Attempting to use the same behavioural model for both scenarios would either make Q unnecessarily slow for simple tasks or insufficiently intelligent for complex decision-making.
The architecture therefore evolved towards a hybrid behavioural model in which Q intelligently switches between different modes of interaction depending on the nature of the user's request.
For operational requests, Q behaves efficiently and directly, completing tasks with minimal conversational overhead.
For strategic discussions, Q transitions into a collaborative analytical mode, helping users evaluate alternatives, understand trade-offs, explore assumptions, and make better-informed decisions.
Importantly, the objective is not to make Q critical for the sake of criticism, nor to discourage founders or investors.
Instead, Q exists to improve decision quality while remaining supportive, constructive, and commercially aligned with helping users succeed.
The discussion also rejected the idea of positioning Q as a "devil's advocate" by default. While challenging assumptions can improve thinking, constant opposition would create unnecessary friction and negatively affect the user experience.
Instead, Q should challenge ideas only when doing so materially improves the quality of the user's decision.
The team also recognised a significant commercial consideration.
If Q were designed to provide unlimited strategic consulting equivalent to human advisory services, it could unintentionally cannibalise future premium services and diminish the value of specialised execution support.
Rather than artificially limiting Q's intelligence, the architecture instead separates diagnosis, intelligence, recommendations, and implementation.
Q is therefore expected to provide substantial value through analysis and recommendations while recognising that implementation, execution, negotiation, and specialist judgement remain distinct human activities.
This philosophy ensures that Q becomes trusted because it is genuinely helpful rather than because it withholds information.

Chapter 5 – Decision #81
Decision
Q Shall Present Strategic Decisions Using a Structured Decision Framework That Combines Options, Trade-offs, Recommendations, Reasoning, and Explicit Assumptions
Rationale
During the design of Q's intelligence philosophy, an important architectural question emerged:
Should Q simply provide answers, or should it guide users through the decision-making process?
The team recognised that many of the decisions founders and investors make are not binary.
Questions such as:
Should I raise capital now or later?
Which investors should I prioritise?
Should I increase my valuation?
Should I expand into another market?
Should I invest in this company?
rarely have universally correct answers.
Instead, they involve balancing competing priorities, uncertainty, opportunity cost, timing, and risk.
Initially, several behavioural models were considered.
One approach involved Q providing direct answers.
While this offered speed and simplicity, it risked creating overdependence on AI while oversimplifying complex strategic decisions.
Another approach involved Q refusing to make recommendations altogether, instead presenting information without guidance.
This was rejected because it reduced Q to little more than an intelligent search engine or knowledge base.
The team ultimately agreed that Q should educate users before guiding them.
Rather than immediately recommending a course of action, Q should first present the available options together with the advantages, disadvantages, and trade-offs associated with each.
Only after establishing this context should Q provide its own recommendation.
Importantly, recommendations should never appear absolute.
Instead, every recommendation should clearly explain:
Why the recommendation is being made.
Which evidence supports it.
Which assumptions the recommendation depends upon.
Under what circumstances the recommendation may change.
This approach creates transparency while preserving human ownership of the final decision.
It also reinforces Q's identity as an analytical partner rather than an automated decision-maker.
The discussion further recognised that not every interaction requires this level of sophistication.
Simple operational requests such as locating documents, opening pages, summarising files, or answering factual questions should continue to receive direct responses.
The structured decision framework is therefore reserved primarily for strategic, high-impact decisions where thoughtful reasoning creates meaningful value.
Alternatives Considered
Option A – Direct Answers
Q simply provides a recommendation or answer without presenting alternatives.
Pros
Fast.
Easy to understand.
Low interaction cost.
Cons
Oversimplifies complex decisions.
Encourages overreliance on AI.
Provides limited educational value.
Reduces transparency.
Rejected.
Option B – Present Trade-offs Only
Q presents multiple options together with their advantages and disadvantages but deliberately avoids making recommendations.
Pros
Encourages independent thinking.
Avoids influencing users excessively.
Cons
Places the entire burden of interpretation on users.
Makes Q feel passive.
Risks becoming similar to a search engine rather than an intelligent analytical partner.
Rejected as a standalone model.
Option C – Recommendations with Reasoning
Q presents options, evaluates evidence, recommends a preferred course of action, explains its reasoning, and identifies the assumptions underlying its recommendation.
Pros
Educates users before advising them.
Maintains transparency.
Improves decision quality.
Reinforces trust.
Preserves human ownership of final decisions.
Adopted.
Option D – Information Only
Q simply presents facts without recommendations or interpretation.
Pros
Completely neutral.
Cons
Adds limited strategic value.
Undermines Q's purpose as an analytical partner.
Rejected.
Final Conclusion
For strategic decisions, Q shall adopt a structured decision-support framework consisting of five sequential stages:
Present the available options.
Explain the advantages, disadvantages, and trade-offs of each option.
Recommend a preferred course of action based on the available evidence.
Clearly explain the reasoning supporting the recommendation.
Explicitly state the assumptions and conditions upon which the recommendation depends.
For routine operational requests, Q shall continue responding directly without unnecessarily invoking the full framework.
This adaptive interaction model ensures users receive rapid assistance for simple tasks while benefiting from institutional-quality decision support when making important strategic decisions.
Impact
This decision establishes one of the core reasoning frameworks underpinning Q's intelligence.
It influences:
Strategic conversations.
Investment recommendations.
Fundraising guidance.
Capital strategy discussions.
Explainability architecture.
User trust.
AI prompting.
Recommendation generation.
Most importantly, it ensures that Q teaches users how to think through decisions rather than merely telling them what to do, reinforcing its role as an intelligent investment analytical partner.

without requiring users to repeatedly identify the subject of discussion.

Chapter 5 – Decision #94
Decision
Q Shall Implement a Progressive Autonomy Framework Where Responsibility Is Delegated Gradually Through Explicit User Permissions Rather Than Immediate Full Automation
Rationale
As Q evolved from an intelligent analytical partner into a system capable of executing workflows, an important architectural question emerged:
How much authority should Q have to act on behalf of users?
The team recognised that intelligence alone is insufficient to create an exceptional user experience.
The true value of institutional intelligence is realised when it can reduce workload by executing appropriate actions, not merely recommending them.
However, granting unrestricted autonomy introduces significant risks.
Users must never feel they have lost control of their businesses, investor relationships, communications, or fundraising activities.
Likewise, requiring user approval for every minor action would significantly reduce Q's usefulness and prevent it from becoming a genuine operational partner.
The discussion therefore rejected both extremes.
Instead, the team adopted a progressive autonomy model based upon earned trust.
This philosophy mirrors how relationships develop between people.
When a new employee joins an organisation, every action is reviewed closely.
As competence and trust are demonstrated over time, greater responsibility is delegated.
Q should evolve in exactly the same manner.
Initially, Q should seek approval before performing meaningful actions.
As users become increasingly comfortable with Q's behaviour, they may explicitly delegate additional authority within clearly defined boundaries.
Importantly, autonomy should never be governed by a single global setting.
Different users have different levels of comfort depending on the activity involved.
For example, a founder may happily allow Q to organise documents automatically while preferring to personally approve every investor email.
Likewise, an investor may permit automatic CRM updates while requiring approval before meeting invitations are sent.
The architecture therefore introduces capability-specific permissions rather than platform-wide autonomy.
This preserves user control while allowing Q to gradually become a genuine operational partner.
The discussion also recognised that users should always understand what authority Q currently possesses.
Delegated responsibilities should remain transparent, reviewable and revocable at any time.
This reinforces trust while preventing accidental over-automation.

Chapter 7: Q Intelligence & Product Architecture
Purpose of This Chapter
This chapter defines the intelligence architecture that sits underneath the Capital Q product experience established in Chapter 6. It determines how Q thinks, remembers, coordinates specialised intelligence, evaluates information, manages uncertainty, responds to change, protects information across counterparties, maintains identity continuity, and combines private and external knowledge.
The objective is to ensure that Capital Q behaves as one coherent intelligence system, even though multiple specialised capabilities may operate underneath it.
Why This Chapter Matters
Capital Q cannot become the intelligence and operating layer for private capital if its intelligence is fragmented across disconnected features.
A founder should not experience one version of Q in InvestIQ, another in fundraising and another in diligence. An investor should not receive contradictory intelligence depending on which part of the product they use.
This chapter establishes the architecture required for Q to maintain continuity, context, trust and coherent reasoning across the entire platform.

This chapter covers:
Q's internal intelligence structure
Memory and context architecture
Truth, provenance and contradiction resolution
Autonomy and delegated authority
Intelligence orchestration
Confidence and explainability
Change propagation
Dual-sided intelligence and conflicts
Identity and organisational continuity
External and connected knowledge
The Context Firewall
The Q Intelligence Graph
This chapter does not redefine founder intelligence, investor intelligence, matching intelligence or behavioural learning already established elsewhere. It also does not define engineering implementation choices such as model providers, databases, infrastructure or specific AI vendors.

Create one coherent Q intelligence architecture capable of understanding the private-capital ecosystem across people, organisations, relationships, evidence and time while maintaining appropriate information boundaries, confidence and human control.

Decision #142 — Q Will Model Confidence and Uncertainty Without Manufacturing Precision
Decision
Q will internally model confidence in its information, interpretations, predictions and recommendations while surfacing uncertainty selectively and proportionately.
Q will distinguish between:
Fact
Claim
Estimate
Inference
Prediction
Recommendation
Unknown
User-facing confidence will generally use understandable qualitative levels such as:
High Confidence
Moderate Confidence
Low Confidence
rather than unsupported numerical precision.
Rationale
Capital Q will make factual statements, analytical judgments, predictions and strategic recommendations.
Presenting these with identical certainty would cause users to confuse what Q knows with what Q believes.
Conversely, placing confidence scores and disclaimers everywhere would make the product noisy and create false mathematical authority.
Implications
Confidence belongs to individual conclusions.
Evidence quality, completeness, recency, consistency, relevance and inference depth affect confidence.
Q separates observation from interpretation and recommendation.
Consequential recommendations expose their principal drivers.
Predictions never masquerade as facts.
Q can form explicitly labelled hypotheses.
Confidence changes as evidence changes.
Confidence influences autonomy thresholds.
Q's language naturally reflects epistemic strength.
Q remains decisive where evidence warrants decisiveness.
Users may challenge Q.
Q can revise its conclusion when new evidence warrants it.
Q may maintain its recommendation when evidence still supports it.
Q may disagree with a user while respecting the user's authorised final decision.
A key distinction is:
Fit ≠ Interest ≠ Investment Probability.
A high Capital Q suitability score does not mean an investor will invest.
Numerical investment-probability predictions should not be introduced until Capital Q possesses sufficient outcome data to calibrate them credibly.
