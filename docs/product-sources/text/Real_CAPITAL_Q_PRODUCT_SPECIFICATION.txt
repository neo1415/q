This document defines the functional behaviour of Capital Q.
Where the Product Architecture Decision Log (PADL) defines what architectural decisions have been made, this Product Specification defines how those decisions are implemented throughout the product.
It serves as the authoritative reference for product design, engineering, AI development, testing, and future product evolution.
Every workflow, interface, AI interaction, business rule, and user experience described within this document must remain consistent with the decisions recorded in the PADL.

It defines:

The Investment Intelligence Framework.
The Founder Intelligence Journey.
The Living Investment Profile.

The Investment Intelligence Record.
Subsequent chapters will expand individual product areas in significantly greater detail.
3. Product Philosophy
3.1 What Capital Q Is
Capital Q is an AI-native Investment Intelligence Operating System designed to reduce information asymmetry within private capital markets.
Unlike traditional fundraising platforms, Capital Q does not primarily exist to introduce founders to investors.
Instead, it exists to generate trusted, continuously evolving investment intelligence that enables investors to evaluate businesses more efficiently while helping founders become more investable over time.
Capital Q treats investment intelligence as the platform's primary product.
Everything else exists to support, improve, distribute, or consume that intelligence.
3.2 Core Product Philosophy
Every feature inside Capital Q should contribute to one or more of the following objectives:
Improve the platform's understanding of a business.
Increase investor confidence.
Reduce investment uncertainty.
Improve founder investment readiness.
Strengthen investment matching.
Enable higher-quality investment decisions.
Features that do not contribute meaningfully to these objectives should not be introduced.
3.3 Product Principles
The following principles guide every product decision.
Intelligence Before Documents
Capital Q presents structured investment intelligence before supporting documents.
Users should understand the business before opening the pitch deck.
Evidence Before Opinion
Conclusions should always be supported by evidence.
Where evidence is insufficient, Capital Q communicates uncertainty rather than making unsupported assumptions.
Continuous Understanding
Capital Q continuously improves its understanding of every business.
The platform is never "finished" assessing a company.
Every interaction has the potential to improve investment intelligence.
Investor Trust Above Everything
Whenever product trade-offs arise, investor trust takes priority over growth, engagement, or convenience.
The long-term value of Capital Q depends upon becoming trusted investment infrastructure.
Explainability
Every important assessment should be explainable.
Users should understand:
what Capital Q believes,
why it believes it,
what evidence supports the conclusion,
where uncertainty remains.
Investment Relationships Over Activity
Success is measured by successful investment relationships rather than the volume of introductions, profile views, or meetings generated.
4. Product Transformation
Capital Q creates two simultaneous transformations.
Founder Transformation
Before Capital Q:
A founder builds a business but struggles to understand how investors evaluate it.
After Capital Q:
The founder understands exactly how institutional investors evaluate the company, continuously improves investment readiness, maintains a Living Investment Profile, and becomes discoverable by the right investors.
The transformation is not simply fundraising.
The transformation is becoming an investable business.
Investor Transformation
Before Capital Q:
Investors spend significant time collecting fragmented information, reviewing documents, interviewing founders, and forming an understanding of each business.
After Capital Q:
Investors begin with structured investment intelligence, understand the business before speaking to the founder, evaluate opportunities more efficiently, and make higher-confidence investment decisions.
5. Product Definition
At its core, Capital Q performs four fundamental functions.
Understand
Capital Q develops a deep institutional understanding of every business through interviews, documentation, evidence, and continuous updates.
Evaluate
Capital Q applies the Investment Intelligence Framework to evaluate every business consistently.
Improve
Capital Q identifies weaknesses, explains investment concerns, and helps founders continuously improve their investment readiness.
Connect
Capital Q intelligently connects the right businesses with the right investors based on evidence, quality, and strategic alignment.

Q – The Investment Intelligence Analyst
2.1 Overview
Q is Capital Q's proprietary Investment Intelligence Analyst.
Q is the primary intelligence layer of the platform and is responsible for understanding businesses, generating investment intelligence, supporting investor evaluation, and continuously improving Capital Q's understanding of every company on the platform.
Unlike traditional AI assistants, Q does not exist to answer general-purpose questions.
Q exists to perform institutional-quality investment analysis.
Every interaction between a user and Q should strengthen Capital Q's Investment Intelligence Record.
2.2 Product Objective
Q has one primary objective.
Build the deepest, most accurate institutional understanding of every private business on Capital Q.
Everything Q does should contribute towards this objective.
Whether Q is:
interviewing founders,
reviewing documents,
answering investor questions,
generating reports,
updating Business Scores,
recommending improvements,
matching companies,
its purpose remains the same.
Q exists to build investment intelligence.

Q has six primary responsibilities.

Q continuously develops an increasingly complete understanding of every business.
This includes understanding:
founders,
products,
markets,

investment readiness,

Q applies the Capital Q Investment Intelligence Framework consistently across every company.
This includes:
Business Scores.
Intelligence Confidence.
Investment Readiness.
Investment Fit.
Risk identification.
Opportunity assessment.

Following assessment, Q explains findings and helps founders understand:
strengths,
weaknesses,

investment concerns,
priority improvements.

Q helps investors understand businesses more quickly by:
answering questions,
generating AI Investment Briefs,
summarising companies,

explaining reasoning.
6. Learn Continuously
Q never considers a business "finished."
Every interaction should improve:

Business Scores,

recommendations,

Q should:

The Capital Q Investment Intelligence Framework (CQIF)
3.1 Overview
The Capital Q Investment Intelligence Framework (CQIF) is the analytical methodology used by Q to evaluate every business within the platform.
The framework provides a universal structure for understanding companies, generating Business Scores, measuring Intelligence Confidence, producing AI Investment Briefs, supporting investor decision-making, and recommending founder improvements.
Rather than functioning as a checklist, the framework represents the structured reasoning process through which Capital Q develops investment-grade understanding.
Every major product capability relies on this framework.
3.2 Product Objective
The objective of the Investment Intelligence Framework is to answer the question:
"Would a well-informed institutional investor understand this business well enough to make a confident investment decision?"
The framework does not decide whether an investor should invest.
Instead, it organises, evaluates, and explains the information required for investors to make that decision themselves.
3.3 Design Principles
The framework operates according to six core principles.
Universal
Every private business is evaluated using the same high-level framework, regardless of sector, geography, or stage.
The underlying assessment criteria adapt to context, but the framework remains consistent.
Evidence-Based
Every meaningful conclusion must be supported by evidence.
Where evidence is unavailable or incomplete, Capital Q communicates reduced Intelligence Confidence rather than presenting assumptions as facts.
Explainable
Every score, recommendation, and conclusion should be explainable.
Users should understand:
what was concluded,
why it was concluded,
what evidence supports it,
what information remains missing.
Dynamic
The framework is continuously updated.
Every interview, document, verification event, company update, investor interaction, or new piece of evidence may refine previous assessments.
Business understanding is never considered complete.
Comparable
Every assessment should enable meaningful comparison between companies while respecting differences in context.
Comparability is essential for investors evaluating multiple opportunities.
Actionable
Every assessment should produce practical insight.
The framework exists not only to evaluate businesses but also to identify the highest-impact opportunities for improvement.
3.4 Assessment Philosophy
The framework evaluates businesses by answering investment questions rather than completing business checklists.
Each assessment dimension exists because it reduces a specific area of investor uncertainty.
The objective is not to gather information.
The objective is to build conviction.
Every question asked during the Founder Intelligence Interview should contribute towards answering one or more investment questions.

3.6 Dual Assessment Model
Every assessment produces two independent outputs.
Business Score
Measures the strength and quality of each assessment dimension.
This score reflects the current state of the business.

Measures Capital Q's confidence in its own assessment.
This score reflects the quality, completeness, reliability, and recency of the available evidence.
A weak Business Score and a low Intelligence Confidence represent different situations and should never be interpreted as the same outcome.

Founder Intelligence Journey
4.1 Overview
The Founder Intelligence Journey is the end-to-end process through which a company becomes understood by Capital Q.
Its purpose is not simply to collect information.
Its purpose is to transform an unknown business into a deeply understood, continuously evolving Investment Intelligence Record that institutional investors can confidently evaluate.
The journey is designed to be iterative rather than linear. A founder should continually return to improve their business, update their information, strengthen their investment readiness, and increase investor confidence over time.
4.2 Product Objective
The Founder Intelligence Journey has four primary objectives:
Understand
Develop a complete institutional understanding of the business.
Evaluate
Assess the business using the Capital Q Investment Intelligence Framework.
Improve
Provide founders with clear, evidence-based recommendations for improving investment readiness.
Represent
Create and continuously maintain the company's Living Investment Profile.
4.3 Guiding Principles
The Founder Intelligence Journey is designed around the following principles.
Founder Time Should Be Respected
Every question asked should have a clear investment purpose.
The platform should never ask for information simply because it is traditionally requested.
Every interaction should contribute directly to building investment intelligence.
Documents Before Questions
Where documents already answer a question, Q should avoid asking the founder to repeat that information.
Instead, Q should:
read,
analyse,
understand,
before beginning the interview.
Questions should clarify, investigate, or expand upon existing information rather than duplicate it.
Every Conversation Should Feel Intelligent
The Founder Intelligence Interview should never resemble a static questionnaire.
It should feel like an experienced investment analyst who has already studied the business and is asking thoughtful follow-up questions.
The founder should feel understood.
Continuous Rather Than One-Time
The journey should never feel complete.
As businesses evolve, founders should naturally return to:
upload updates,
improve documentation,
refresh metrics,
strengthen governance,
update milestones,
improve their Investment Intelligence Record.
4.4 Founder Journey Overview
Every founder progresses through the following stages.
Stage 1
Company Creation
The founder creates a company profile and provides essential organisational information.
Examples include:
Company name.
Industry.
Geography.
Stage.
Website.
Basic business information.
This creates the initial company record.
Stage 2
Initial Document Submission
Before any interview begins, founders upload the documentation required for Capital Q to understand the business.
Typical examples include:
Mandatory:
Pitch deck.
Founder information.
Basic company information.
Stage-dependent examples may include:
Financial model.
Management accounts.
Cap table.
Product demonstrations.
Legal documentation.
Customer metrics.
Data room materials.
The platform should clearly communicate which documents are mandatory, recommended, or optional based on the company's funding stage.
Stage 3
AI Document Analysis
Q analyses every uploaded document before asking the founder a single question.
This analysis should identify:
strengths,
inconsistencies,
missing information,
unsupported claims,
areas requiring clarification,
potential investment concerns.
The resulting understanding becomes the foundation for the Founder Intelligence Interview.
Stage 4
Founder Intelligence Interview
The Founder Intelligence Interview begins only after document analysis has been completed.
The interview combines:
standard investment questions,
adaptive follow-up questions,
document-specific questions,
clarification requests,
evidence gathering,
deeper investigation where necessary.
The objective is understanding rather than interview completion.
Q should continue exploring important topics until sufficient investment intelligence has been gathered.
Stage 5
Assessment
Following the interview, Capital Q evaluates the business using the Investment Intelligence Framework.
Outputs include:
Business Scores.
Intelligence Confidence.
Investment Readiness.
Investment Fit.
Identified strengths.
Investment concerns.
Missing evidence.
No recommendations are generated until assessment has been completed.
Stage 6
Investment Intelligence Debrief
Following assessment, founders receive a comprehensive debrief explaining:
how the business was evaluated,
why scores were assigned,
key strengths,
major investment concerns,
missing evidence,
priority improvement areas,
next recommended actions.
The objective is to educate founders rather than simply inform them.
Stage 7
Continuous Improvement
Following the debrief, founders transition into ongoing platform usage.
Typical activities include:
updating metrics,
uploading new evidence,
refreshing documents,
responding to investor feedback,
improving governance,
strengthening Business Scores,
increasing Intelligence Confidence,
monitoring investor engagement.
This stage has no defined endpoint.
The company continuously evolves.
4.5 Expected Founder Outcomes
Upon completing the Founder Intelligence Journey, every founder should possess:
A Living Investment Profile.
An Investment Intelligence Record.
Business Scores across every assessment dimension.
Intelligence Confidence scores.
An Investment Readiness assessment.
A personalised Investment Intelligence Debrief.
A prioritised improvement roadmap.
Visibility into investor engagement.
Recommendations for improving investment readiness.
Where appropriate:
referrals to FDN Advisory,
referrals to specialist ecosystem partners.
4.6 Success Criteria
The Founder Intelligence Journey is successful when founders:
Understand how institutional investors evaluate businesses.
Trust the accuracy of Capital Q's assessment.
Clearly understand what to improve next.
Regularly return to update their business.
Gradually improve Business Scores.
Gradually improve Intelligence Confidence.
Become increasingly investment-ready.
Ultimately become discoverable by highly aligned investors.

The Founder Intelligence Interview
5.1 Overview
The Founder Intelligence Interview is Capital Q's primary mechanism for understanding a business.
Rather than functioning as a questionnaire or application form, it is designed to replicate the investigative process of an experienced institutional investment analyst.
Its objective is not to complete an interview.
Its objective is to develop the deepest possible understanding of the business.
Every question, clarification, and follow-up exists to improve the quality of Capital Q's Investment Intelligence Record.
5.2 Product Objective
The Founder Intelligence Interview has one primary objective:
Enable Q to understand the business as thoroughly as an experienced institutional investment team would before presenting it to an investment committee.
The interview should produce sufficient intelligence for investors to understand the company before ever speaking with the founder.
5.3 Guiding Principles
The interview is governed by the following principles.
Q Always Does Its Homework First
The interview never begins immediately.
Before asking a single question, Q should:
analyse every uploaded document,
identify strengths,
identify inconsistencies,
identify missing information,
identify unsupported claims,
identify areas requiring clarification,
build an initial understanding of the company.
Only then should questioning begin.
The founder should immediately feel that Q already understands the business.
Questions Must Have Purpose
Every question should exist because it helps answer an investment question.
Q should never ask questions simply to collect information.
Every question should either:
strengthen understanding,
reduce uncertainty,
validate evidence,
investigate inconsistencies,
or improve investor confidence.
The Conversation Should Feel Natural
The Founder Intelligence Interview should resemble a thoughtful conversation rather than a structured survey.
Q should:
ask one meaningful question at a time,
actively listen,
adapt naturally,
revisit earlier topics when necessary,
avoid unnecessary repetition.
The founder should feel interviewed rather than processed.
Understanding Takes Priority Over Completion
The objective is not to finish quickly.
The objective is to understand accurately.
If an important area remains unclear, Q should continue investigating until sufficient understanding has been achieved.
5.4 Interview Structure
Although every interview is dynamic, the overall structure remains consistent.
Phase 1 – Preparation
Q reviews all submitted information.
Outputs include:
Initial company understanding.
Missing information.
Clarification opportunities.
Potential risks.
Areas requiring deeper investigation.
Phase 2 – Core Interview
Q conducts a structured conversation covering the core Investment Intelligence dimensions.
This ensures every company is evaluated consistently.
Phase 3 – Adaptive Investigation
Following the core interview, Q explores company-specific topics generated from:
uploaded documents,
founder responses,
identified inconsistencies,
missing evidence,
unusual business characteristics,
investment risks.
No two adaptive interviews should be identical.
Phase 4 – Validation
Before concluding the interview, Q validates its understanding.
Typical examples include:
confirming assumptions,
resolving contradictions,
requesting additional evidence,
confirming timelines,
verifying important facts.
The objective is confidence rather than speed.
Phase 5 – Completion
Only once Q determines sufficient investment intelligence has been gathered should the interview conclude.
Assessment begins only after interview completion.
5.5 Question Generation
Questions originate from three sources.
Universal Questions
Questions asked of every company.
These establish a consistent assessment baseline.
Contextual Questions
Questions generated from:
uploaded documents,
company stage,
sector,
geography,
business model,
fundraising objectives.
These personalise every interview.
Investigative Questions
Questions generated dynamically whenever Q identifies:
inconsistencies,
unsupported claims,
missing evidence,
unusual performance,
unclear assumptions,
potential investment risks.
These questions continue until sufficient understanding is achieved.
5.6 Evidence Collection
Throughout the interview, Q continuously gathers evidence.
Evidence may include:
founder responses,
uploaded documents,
financial information,
product demonstrations,
governance documents,
customer information,
historical updates,
investor feedback.
Every significant conclusion should be traceable to one or more pieces of supporting evidence.
5.7 Interview Completion Criteria
The interview should conclude only when Q believes it has sufficient information to:
Generate Business Scores.
Generate Intelligence Confidence.
Produce the Investment Intelligence Record.
Generate the Investment Intelligence Debrief.
Populate the Living Investment Profile.
Support future investor evaluation.
If important uncertainty remains, Q should either:
continue questioning, or
request additional documentation before assessment.
5.8 Founder Experience
By the end of the interview, founders should feel:
"I've just had one of the most thorough investment conversations I've ever had."
They should believe that:
Q genuinely understands their business.
The questions were thoughtful.
The conversation felt personalised.
Nothing important was overlooked.
The resulting assessment will accurately represent their company.
5.9 Success Criteria
The Founder Intelligence Interview is successful when:
founders feel understood,
investors require fewer introductory questions,
Business Scores accurately represent the business,
Intelligence Confidence is high,
the Investment Intelligence Record is comprehensive,
the Living Investment Profile reflects the business accurately,
future conversations become progressively shorter because Q already understands the company.

Investment Intelligence Debrief & Improvement Engine
6.1 Overview
The Investment Intelligence Debrief is the process through which Capital Q transforms its internal assessment into actionable intelligence for the founder.
The purpose of the debrief is not merely to communicate scores.
Its purpose is to explain how institutional investors are likely to evaluate the business, identify the factors affecting investment readiness, and provide a clear roadmap for improvement.
The debrief marks the transition from assessment to continuous improvement.
6.2 Product Objective
The Investment Intelligence Debrief has four primary objectives.
Explain
Help founders understand how Capital Q evaluated the business.
Educate
Help founders understand how institutional investors think.
Prioritise
Identify the highest-impact improvements that will increase investment readiness.
Activate
Encourage founders to take the next step, whether that involves improving independently, engaging FDN Advisory, or working with trusted ecosystem partners.
6.3 Guiding Principles
Every Conclusion Must Be Explainable
Founders should never receive a score without understanding:
why it was assigned,
what evidence supports it,
what evidence is missing,
how it can be improved.
The debrief should educate rather than simply report.
Prioritise Impact
Not all weaknesses are equally important.
The debrief should identify the improvements most likely to increase investor confidence and investment readiness.
Recommendations should be prioritised by expected impact rather than listed equally.
Separate Weakness from Uncertainty
A low Business Score and a low Intelligence Confidence score represent different situations.
The debrief should clearly distinguish between:
areas where the business genuinely needs improvement, and
areas where additional evidence is required before a confident assessment can be made.
This distinction is essential for helping founders focus their efforts appropriately.
Recommendations Must Be Actionable
Every recommendation should answer:
What is the issue?
Why does it matter to investors?
What should the founder do next?
Who can help solve it?
Recommendations should always lead to a practical next step.
6.4 Debrief Structure
The Investment Intelligence Debrief should follow a consistent structure.
Executive Summary
A concise overview of the business.
This should include:
overall Investment Readiness,
overall Business Score,
overall Intelligence Confidence,
strongest investment strengths,
highest-priority concerns,
overall assessment.
Business Score Breakdown
Each Investment Intelligence dimension should display:
Business Score,
Intelligence Confidence,
supporting explanation,
evidence considered,
identified concerns.
This allows founders to understand their performance across every assessment area.
Investment Readiness Assessment
Capital Q should explain:
whether the company is currently investment-ready,
what type of capital it is currently prepared for,
what limitations prevent access to the next level of capital,
what improvements would unlock future fundraising opportunities.
Priority Improvement Roadmap
The debrief should identify the highest-impact actions in order of priority.
Each recommendation should include:
issue identified,
investor impact,
recommended action,
expected benefit.
This roadmap becomes the founder's immediate action plan.
Missing Evidence
Capital Q should identify where stronger evidence would increase Intelligence Confidence.
Examples include:
financial reporting,
customer validation,
governance documentation,
legal information,
commercial metrics.
The platform should make it clear that improving evidence quality is often as valuable as improving the business itself.
Recommended Support
Where appropriate, Capital Q should recommend:
FDN Advisory
For strategic investment readiness support.
Examples include:
fundraising strategy,
governance,
investor materials,
financial preparation,
investment positioning.
Ecosystem Partners
Where specialist expertise is required.
Examples include:
branding,
legal,
accounting,
technology,
product,
recruitment,
marketing.
Recommendations should always be directly linked to identified weaknesses.
6.5 Continuous Improvement Engine
The Investment Intelligence Debrief should not function as a static report.
Instead, it should become the founder's ongoing improvement dashboard.
As founders update their business, the platform should:
refresh recommendations,
track completed improvements,
update Business Scores,
update Intelligence Confidence,
measure progress,
identify new priorities.
The roadmap should evolve as the business evolves.
6.6 Founder Actions
Following the debrief, founders should be able to:
upload additional evidence,
update company information,
improve documentation,
complete recommended actions,
engage FDN Advisory,
engage ecosystem partners,
refresh assessments,
monitor investor engagement,
continue strengthening their Living Investment Profile.
The debrief should become the central hub for continuous investment readiness.
6.7 Success Criteria
The Investment Intelligence Debrief is successful when founders:
understand exactly how investors see their business,
trust the assessment,
know precisely what to improve next,
return regularly to strengthen their company,
increase both Business Scores and Intelligence Confidence over time,
ultimately become more attractive to institutional investors.
The founder should finish the debrief with one clear thought:
"For the first time, I understand exactly how investors see my business and exactly what I need to do next."

Section 7
The Living Investment Profile
7.1 Overview
The Living Investment Profile is the primary investment representation of every company within Capital Q.
It replaces static fundraising materials with a continuously evolving, AI-generated investment intelligence profile that reflects the company's current state.
Unlike a traditional pitch deck, which represents a single moment in time, the Living Investment Profile evolves continuously as new information, evidence, assessments, investor interactions, and company updates are incorporated into the Investment Intelligence Record.
The Living Investment Profile serves as the primary interface through which investors evaluate businesses.
7.2 Product Objective
The Living Investment Profile has five primary objectives.
Represent
Provide a complete, accurate, and continuously updated representation of the business.
Explain
Enable investors to understand the business without first reading multiple documents.
Build Trust
Present structured investment intelligence supported by transparent evidence.
Improve Discovery
Enable high-quality matching between founders and investors.
Evolve

6.0 Chapter Overview
Purpose of This Chapter
This chapter defines how Capital Q works as an actual product from the perspective of founders, investors and other authorised participants.
Previous chapters establish Capital Q's underlying philosophy, users, objectives, intelligence principles and operating rules. Chapter 6 translates those foundations into the principal experiences, workflows and interactions that users will encounter inside the platform.
This chapter therefore answers questions such as:
What does a founder see when they enter Capital Q?
What does an investor see?
How does Q interact with each user?
How are companies and investors discovered?
How does GateQ control investor inbound?
How does InvestIQ work from beginning to completion?
What happens after a company receives its assessment?
How does the Capital Readiness Blueprint operate?
How does fundraising actually happen inside Capital Q?
What constitutes a Match?
How do meetings, messaging and diligence work?
How is sensitive information controlled?
How does Capital Q track fundraising progress?
What happens after an investment is completed?
What intelligence should Q surface to the founder?
The purpose is not to prescribe every pixel, screen or technical implementation. Detailed UI/UX design will follow from this specification.
Instead, this chapter establishes the functional behaviour that product designers and engineers must preserve when implementing Capital Q.
Why This Chapter Matters
Capital Q contains significant underlying complexity.
It may eventually understand companies, investors, capital objectives, investment mandates, behaviour, documents, meetings, relationships, diligence, fundraising progress and historical outcomes.
The user should not experience that complexity.
The governing principle for the entire platform experience is:
Q manages complexity. The user sees clarity.
Capital Q should not feel like several products stitched together.
InvestIQ, Blueprint, Discover, GateQ, Matching, Fundraising, Meetings and Data Room should operate as connected components of one capital operating environment.
A founder should not need to repeatedly provide the same information to different Capital Q modules.
An investor should not need to reconstruct a company manually from fragmented information.
Q should understand context across the platform and use that context appropriately, subject to permissions and privacy.

Capital Q should create an interconnected journey that can support:
Understand → Assess → Improve → Discover → Match → Connect → Meet → Diligence → Raise

Financial model
Business plan
Website

Corporate information

Contradictions
Material assumptions

Voice where enabled

It is:

6.10.6 Negative Signal Architecture
Negative fundraising signals should be communicated through a structured reasoning hierarchy.
Layer 1: What Happened
Objective observation.
9 investor meetings have resulted in 0 diligence processes.
Layer 2: What Q Observes
Evidence available to Capital Q.
Financial performance was raised as a concern in 5 of the 7 investor conversations for which sufficient feedback is available.
Layer 3: What Q Thinks It Means
Interpretation.
Investors may not yet be convinced that current traction supports the proposed valuation and raise size.
Layer 4: What Q Recommends
Action.
Review the valuation narrative and financial positioning before the next investor meetings.
This architecture should be used wherever Q is drawing conclusions from behavioural patterns rather than merely reporting known facts.
6.10.7 Confidence
Q should appropriately communicate confidence in its interpretations.
Potential levels:
High Confidence
Strong, repeated and sufficiently direct evidence.
Moderate Confidence
Meaningful evidence exists, but alternative explanations remain plausible.
Low Confidence
Q is primarily inferring from limited signals.
Confidence should relate to the specific conclusion, not become a decorative badge attached to every sentence.
6.10.8 Negative Signals Must Not Automatically Stop Fundraising
If Q identifies a problem, its default question should be:
How do we improve the probability of success?
Not:
How do we stop the founder from fundraising?
For example:
9 meetings → 0 diligence
should trigger investigation, diagnosis and strategy adjustment.
It should not automatically disable Discover, GateQ access, Matches or fundraising activity.
This maintains the principle established throughout Chapter 6:
Readiness exists to improve fundraising outcomes. Fundraising does not exist as a reward for achieving theoretical readiness.

6.12 PRODUCT-WIDE EXPERIENCE RULES
The following rules apply across the Chapter 6 experience.
Rule 1 — Q Manages Complexity
The underlying system can be sophisticated.

Rule 2 — Q Is Central, Not Mandatory
Q should be deeply integrated into the product without forcing every user action through conversation.
Rule 3 — Capital Formation Is Bilateral
Capital Q must create value for both businesses and capital providers.
Rule 4 — Protect Investor Attention
Founder access cannot be solved by creating investor spam.
Rule 5 — Interest Is Unilateral; Match Is Bilateral
Mutual consent creates the formal capital relationship.
Rule 6 — Q Handles Information; Humans Handle Relationships
Q should remove information friction without unnecessarily inserting itself between people.
Rule 7 — Meetings Matter More Than Messages
Capital Q should optimise relationships toward meaningful investment conversations.
Rule 8 — Readiness and Fundraising Can Operate in Parallel
A company does not need to become theoretically perfect before entering the market.
Rule 9 — Outcome Over Perfection
Capital Q exists to improve capital outcomes, not maximise the number of tasks founders complete.
Rule 10 — Sensitive Information Is Permissioned
Q knowing something does not mean another user is entitled to know it.
Rule 11 — Evidence and Inference Are Different
Q must distinguish between:
Known → Supported → Founder-Provided → Assumed/Estimated → Unknown → Q Interpretation
These states must not silently collapse into one another.
Rule 12 — Consequential Actions Require Appropriate Control
Q can become increasingly capable without removing human authority over sensitive decisions.
Rule 13 — Learn Without Creating Surveillance
Capital Q should use behavioural information to improve intelligence while avoiding unnecessary founder-facing monitoring of investor browsing behaviour.
Rule 14 — Preserve Capital History
Matches, meetings, diligence, passes, investments and completed raises should contribute to long-term relationship and outcome intelligence.
Rule 15 — Do Not Build Adjacent Products Without Necessity
Capital Q should resist becoming:
A generic CRM
Asana
Slack
Zoom
A cap-table platform
A generic Data Room
A board-management system
It should integrate, abstract or selectively build functionality where that functionality directly improves the capital journey.

7.0 Chapter Overview
Purpose of This Chapter
Chapter 7 defines the underlying intelligence architecture that allows Capital Q to behave as one coherent system across the entire platform.
Chapter 6 defined the user-facing product experience.
Chapter 7 defines what must happen beneath that experience so Q can:
remember information correctly
understand context
coordinate specialised intelligence

manage uncertainty

Capital Q is not simply a collection of AI features.

A product operating this way would lose trust very quickly.
Chapter 7 therefore establishes the common intelligence architecture underneath the entire Capital Q platform.

7.3 Truth, Provenance & Contradiction Resolution
Product Objective
Ensure Q understands the difference between reported information, evidence, estimates, inference and verified facts.
Knowledge States
Q should conceptually support the following states.
Verified
Strong authoritative or independent evidence supports the claim.
Document-Supported
A provided document supports the information.
This does not necessarily mean Capital Q independently verified the underlying truth.
User-Provided
An authorised person supplied the information.
Estimated / Assumed
The value depends on assumptions or projections.
Inferred
Q derived the conclusion from available information.
Unknown
Q lacks enough information.
Disputed / Contradictory
Material sources cannot currently be reconciled.
Contradiction Detection
Q should not identify two numbers as contradictory without understanding context.
It should consider:
period
currency
metric definition
forecast vs actual
gross vs net
standalone vs consolidated
historical vs current
accounting treatment
estimates vs recognised results
Example
Source A:
FY2025 revenue: $2.7M.
Source B:
Current ARR: $3.2M.
These are not automatically contradictory.
Q should understand what each number measures.
Material Contradiction Workflow
Step 1: Reconcile Automatically
Determine whether the apparent contradiction can be explained.
Step 2: Assess Materiality
Determine whether the difference affects important conclusions.
Step 3: Request Clarification
If necessary:
“Your July management accounts show $2.7M while your current profile states $3.2M. Are these measuring the same revenue period and definition?”
Step 4: Update Knowledge State
If resolved, store the appropriate contextual truth.
If unresolved, retain:
Contradictory / unresolved
rather than deleting one source.
Correction History
Q should never silently rewrite prior history.
Example:
Original statement:
ARR = $2.8M
Later correction:
ARR = $2.3M
Q should understand:
$2.8M was previously represented.
The value was later corrected.
$2.3M is the current accepted value.
Earlier downstream outputs may require review.
Unknown Information
Q should be able to answer:
“I don't currently have sufficient information to determine that.”
Where useful, Q should then identify what would resolve the uncertainty.
Product Rules
Different sources do not have equal authority.
Recency does not automatically beat stronger evidence.
Formal evidence does not automatically beat newer information when the question concerns current state.
Q preserves conflicting claims where unresolved.
Q never invents certainty.
Material corrections can trigger reassessment elsewhere.

The user controls this contact point.
It does not establish:
The user's claimed professional identity is true.
Level 2 — Individual Identity Verified
Capital Q has established sufficient confidence that the person is who they claim to be.
Specific verification mechanisms will be determined during implementation and may include appropriate identity-verification providers and documentation.
Level 3 — Organisation Affiliation Verified
Capital Q has established sufficient confidence that:
This person is legitimately affiliated with this organisation.
Potential evidence may include:
verified corporate domain;
invitation by authorised organisation administrator;
employment/affiliation verification;
appropriate organisation records;
other approved evidence.
Individual identity and organisation affiliation remain separate verification claims.
Level 4 — Organisation Verified
Capital Q has established sufficient confidence in the organisation itself.
Potential verification dimensions may include:
legal existence;
official organisation identity;
official domain;
authorised administrators;
jurisdiction;
registration information;
relevant fund/company information.
Exact requirements may differ between company types.
8.1.3 Verification Is Claim-Specific
Capital Q must avoid the generic concept:
Verified User
Instead, verification attaches to specific claims.
For example:
Identity Verified
means Capital Q has verified the individual's identity.
Organisation Verified
means Capital Q has verified the organisation.
Affiliation Verified
means Capital Q has verified the relationship between the individual and organisation.
These claims do not automatically establish:
investment authorityfinancial capacityprofessional competenceinvestment qualitytrustworthinessendorsement by Capital Q
8.1.4 Investment Authority Is Separate
For investors in particular:
Works at Apex Ventures
is not equivalent to:
Has authority to commit capital on behalf of Apex Ventures.
Capital Q should not represent investment authority as verified unless it has a legitimate mechanism for establishing that claim.
8.1.5 Action-Based Verification

Conceptually:

Exact thresholds will be defined during implementation.
The architecture principle is:

8.1.7 Organisation Claiming

remove members;
assign roles;
manage organisation-level settings.

8.11 Product-Wide Trust Rules
The following rules apply across Capital Q.
Rule 1
Knowing does not equal permission to disclose.
Rule 2
If a user cannot access information directly, they cannot bypass that restriction through Q.
Rule 3
Identity, organisation, affiliation and authority are separate claims.
Rule 4
Verification is not endorsement.
Rule 5
Sensitive information defaults toward private.
Rule 6
Derived intelligence inherits appropriate sensitivity from its sources.
Rule 7
Personal-private, organisation-private and counterparty-private contexts remain isolated.
Rule 8
Direct processing, private learning, network learning and model training are different data uses.
Rule 9
Q should not create hidden cross-network reputational blacklists from confidential subjective opinions.
Rule 10
Deletion rights follow ownership and context.
Rule 11
Revocation does not falsely imply retrieval of external copies.
Rule 12
Historical truth should not be silently rewritten.
Rule 13
Consequential Q actions require attributable authority.
Rule 14
More consequential actions require stronger verification, permissions, confirmation and auditability.
Rule 15
Reports and suspicious signals do not automatically establish guilt.
Rule 16
Capital Q protects network quality rather than maximising messaging volume.
Rule 17
Q may strongly challenge commercial decisions without automatically taking those decisions away from authorised users.
Rule 18
Integrity restrictions cannot be bypassed through Q.
Rule 19
Commercial incentives cannot secretly alter objective Q intelligence.
Rule 20
Trust complexity belongs underneath the product. Users should experience clarity.

The user does not need to manage that complexity.
That is the intended architecture.
8.13 Definition of Success
Chapter 8 succeeds when Capital Q can satisfy all of the following simultaneously:
1. Users can trust Q with sensitive information.
They understand that telling Q something does not automatically tell counterparties.
2. Organisations retain meaningful control.
They can determine who sees and does what without constructing an enterprise permission system from scratch.
3. Q cannot become a permissions loophole.
AI access follows the same information boundaries as direct access.
4. Capital Q can learn without collapsing confidentiality.
Private intelligence, organisation learning, protected network intelligence and model training remain architecturally distinct.
5. Users retain meaningful control over memory.
They can correct, revoke, forget, delete, export and leave according to legitimate ownership and retention rules.
6. The network resists degradation.
Spam, impersonation, scams, Data Room harvesting and abusive behaviour encounter progressively stronger friction.
7. Capital Q does not confuse risk signals with guilt.
Integrity systems remain evidence-aware and proportionate.
8. Q actions remain accountable.
Consequential activity can be attributed, explained and reconstructed.
9. Q protects hard trust boundaries without taking over legitimate commercial judgement.
The founder remains the founder.
The investor remains the investor.
Q remains the intelligence and operating layer.
10. The product still feels simple.
Users should experience:
“Q handles it.”
rather than:
“I need to understand Capital Q's permission architecture.”
