# FDN InvestIQ v1: research basis

Status: research notes for `investiq/v1` ("owner-delegated, pending calibration"), 2026-10-15, Lane M. The owner delegated weights, rules and bands to the lead ("do deep research and pick the best"). Each v1 parameter below cites the sources it rests on. **Where a number is a judgment, it says so.** None of these sources calibrates an African/GCC private-capital readiness index. They inform structure and relative emphasis; calibration (`CALIBRATION.md`) has to validate the numbers. Retrieved content is treated as data. Secondary sources are marked.

## Sources

| ID | Source | What it establishes | Used for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| R1 | Payne Scorecard method (W. H. Payne, Ohio TechAngels; Kauffman-endorsed). [Venionaire summary](https://www.venionaire.com/?p=14038); [Equidam](https://support.equidam.com/valuation/approaches-and-methods/qualitative-methods-scorecard); [Esteban](https://esteban.bearblog.dev/scorecard-startup-valuation-method/); [SlideShare citing Payne 2006](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/5-methods-to-value-a-business/71454882) | Factor weights relative to a norm of funded peers ("the norm … is 100%"). Commonly cited caps: management team 30%, size of opportunity 20–25%, product/technology 15%, competitive environment 5–10%, sales channels/partnerships 5–10%, need for additional investment 0–5%, other 5%. Versions differ (secondary sources); weights are investor-adjustable. | Pre-seed team weight 30; centre of the anchor scale = "the norm"; fundamentals (market + product + competition) as the next-largest block. |
| R2 | Berkus method. [Allied VC](https://www.allied.vc/guides/berkus-method-vs-other-valuation-models); [Hustle Fund](https://www.hustlefund.vc/post/berkus-method); [berkus.com](https://berkus.com/?p=131) | Five pre-revenue risk elements (sound idea, prototype/technology, quality management, strategic relationships, product rollout/sales), each capped, partial credit allowed. Berkus calls the matrix "a suggestion rather than a rigid form". | Partial-credit anchors; commercial evidence weighted low before revenue; IP and prototype under fundamentals. |
| R3 | Risk Factor Summation. [Rho](https://www.rho.co/blog/risk-factor-summation-method); [Eqvista](https://eqvista.com/risk-factor-summation-method/); [CFI](https://corporatefinanceinstitute.com/resources/valuation/startup-valuation-methods) | Twelve risks (management, stage, legislation/political, manufacturing, sales/marketing, funding, competition, technology, litigation, international, reputation, exit), each scored −2..+2 around a regional baseline. | **Anchor scale 0–4 = RFS −2..+2 shifted by +2**; 2 = baseline. Funding + exit (2 of 12, ~17%) inform an Investment Case weight of ~10%; legislation and litigation inform Legal & Corporate. |
| R4 | Investment Readiness Level (Steve Blank; NSF I-Corps / LaunchPad Central). [Blank 2013](https://steveblank.com/2013/11/25/its-time-to-play-moneyball-the-investment-readiness-level/); [Blank 2014](https://steveblank.com/2014/02/25/is-this-startup-ready-for-investment/amp/) | Readiness measured from *evidence of validated hypotheses* (customer interviews, invalidated hypotheses), not presentation impressions. It has sector variants (therapeutics, devices: regulation, reimbursement, IP, exit). The level definitions are not in the posts (graphic only). | Evidence-over-claims multiplier; readiness rises with commercial validation, so commercial weight grows with stage; sector-module hook. |
| R5 | Gompers, Gornall, Kaplan, Strebulaev, "How Do Venture Capitalists Make Decisions?" (JFE 2020; NBER w22587; 885–889 VCs). [HLS Forum summary](https://corpgov.law.harvard.edu/2019/08/20/how-do-venture-capitalists-make-decisions/); [NBER](https://m.nber.org/digest/dec16/w22587.html) | Team was named important by 95% of firms and most important by 47%. Business model was important to 83%, product 74%, market 68%. Valuation is 5th overall but 3rd for later-stage deals. | Team + fundamentals dominate early; valuation kept out of the grade (it matters to price, not readiness). |
| R6 | Mason, Botelho, Zygmunt, angel rejection reasons (ISBJ; 30 interviews, 238 UK angels). [UEA eprint](https://ueaeprints.uea.ac.uk/id/eprint/58608/1/Mason_et_al_ISBJ_Revised_FINAL.pdf); Croce et al., Italian angel groups, 1,942 ventures. [UniUPO](https://research.uniupo.it/it/publications/how-business-angel-groups-work-rejection-criteria-in-investment-e/) | The main rejection reason is the entrepreneur/management team. Behaviour that damages trust is rejected. Lower profitability is more often rejected *after due diligence*. | Team weight; Team + Fundamentals required for an overall band; financial weight grows at diligence stages. |
| R7 | European Commission (2006) and Mason & Harrison (2001) on SME investment readiness. [EC discussion paper](https://ec.europa.eu/docsroom/documents/3326/attachments/1/translations/en/renditions/pdf); [Mason & Harrison 2001, Regional Studies](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/regstd/v35y2001i7p663-668.html); [Inderscience review](https://inderscience.com/offer.php?id=74904) | Readiness is the ability to understand investor needs and respond with appropriate structure, relevant information, credibility and trust. Programmes cover business plan, financing sources, investor requirements, management skills, presentation. No OECD-specific readiness index was found. | Investment Readiness as its own output (structure, information, credibility); diligence readiness = "relevant information". |
| R8 | IFC Corporate Governance Methodology (Progression Matrix, four levels by maturity; SME and family/founder-owned variants) and IFC E&S categorisation (A/B/C) with the exclusion list applied first. [IFC CG Methodology](https://www.ifc.org/en/what-we-do/sector-expertise/corporate-governance/cg-methodology-tools); [IFC E&S categorization](https://www.ifc.org/en/what-we-do/sector-expertise/sustainability/policies-and-standards/environmental-and-social-categorization); [CDC ESG toolkit](https://assets.cdcgroup.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/09/08155015/CDC_Toolkit_on_ESG_for_Fund_Managers_2010.pdf) | Governance expectations scale with company maturity (Level 1 basic → Level 4 leadership). E&S screening is categorical and exclusion-first, not a weighted score. For SMEs, ESG improvements are prioritised. | Governance and legal weights grow with stage; **E&S/exclusion screening is a gate, not a weight**: proposed as a v1.1 hard flag (not implemented; open question). |
| R9 | VC diligence checklists: YC Series A checklist (18 material-agreement items, 7 securities items, a 2-line financial section); Sequoia business-plan outline (purpose, problem, solution, why now, market, competition, product, business model, team, financials). [YC blog](https://blog.ycombinator.com/ycs-series-a-diligence-checklist); [Sequoia outline (secondary)](https://www.alexanderjarvis.com/sequoia-capital-business-plan-template/); [Pitchwise (DocSend 3.5-week figure, secondary)](https://www.pitchwise.se/blog/series-a-due-diligence-checklist-what-investors-will-ask-for-2026) | What investors actually request: legal and cap-table documents dominate confirmatory diligence; business diligence precedes the term sheet. No a16z-specific checklist was found. | Diligence Readiness criteria and their growth with stage; Legal & Corporate weight at growth. |
| R10 | Moody's rating methodology scorecards. [Moody's methodology docs](https://ratings.moodys.com/api/rmc-documents/70054); [Vermont summary of 2018 US States (secondary)](https://www.vermonttreasurer.gov/sites/treasurer/files/Moodys_State%20Methodology%202018.pdf) | Weighted sub-factor scores mapped to an alphanumeric outcome by ranges. Example weights: Economy 25 / Governance 20 / Finances 30 / Debt 25. The "scorecard-indicated outcome is not expected to match the actual rating". | Weighted mean then **mapped to bands by ranges**; bands advise, humans decide; financial weight largest for mature entities. |
| R11 | GRADE certainty of evidence. [BMJ Best Practice](https://bestpractice.bmj.com/info/toolkit/learn-ebm/what-is-grade) | Four discrete levels (high, moderate, low, very low). Evidence starts at a level by type and is downgraded for risk of bias, inconsistency, indirectness, imprecision. Judgment is acknowledged. | **Discrete confidence levels**; evidence-type multiplier; one-level downgrade for inconsistency (open conflicts). PADL #142 limits user-facing levels to High/Moderate/Low. |
| R12 | Locked Capital Q sources: PADL Ch4 #64 (dynamic weighting by stage/sector/model/evidence), #65 (evidence hierarchy; volume is not strength), #67 (conflicts investigated, not penalised), #68 (missing ≠ zero), #74 (no capital-form prescription), #142 (no false precision); FSR §4 (Readiness ≠ Quality ≠ Fit). | Constraints the method must respect. | Stage weights, no volume bonus, conflicts withheld, coverage gates, Investor Fit routed out, valuation ungraded. |

## v1 parameters and their basis

| Parameter | v1 value | Basis | Kind |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Anchor scale | 0–4, 2 = stage-appropriate baseline | R3 (−2..+2), R1 (100% norm) | sourced structure |
| Default anchors | weakness 1, recorded 2, strength 3; explicit 0/4 need a rubric anchor | R2 partial credit; R3 | judgment |
| Evidence multiplier w | NO_EVIDENCE .25, SELF_REPORTED .5, DOCUMENT_SUPPORTED .75, MULTI_SOURCE .85, EXTERNALLY/PLATFORM_VERIFIED 1 | R11 start-by-type and downgrade; R4 evidence over impressions; R12 #65 | judgment (ordinal from R11; spacing to calibrate) |
| Truth multiplier | VERIFIED/USER_CLAIM 1, ESTIMATE .75, Q_INFERENCE .5 (evidence-backed only) | R11 indirectness; ADR-001 axes | judgment |
| Lifecycle | STALE ×.5; superseded excluded; contradictory/disputed → criterion withheld | R11; R12 #67 | judgment |
| Criterion score | `2 + (anchor − 2) × w` over the highest-w observations (no volume bonus) | R12 #65; R10 data quality limits outcome | judgment |
| Stage weights | see the table in `method-v1.ts` (Team 30→15, Fundamentals 30→10, Commercial 10→20, Financial 5→20, Governance 5→10, Legal 5→10, Investment Case 10→5, Diligence 5→10) | R1, R5, R6 (team first); R4, R9, R10 (commercial, financial and diligence rise); R3 (funding + exit); R8 (governance by maturity) | judgment informed by sources |
| Bands | NOT_YET_READY < 1.5 ≤ DEVELOPING < 2.0 ≤ READY < 2.75 ≤ STRONG | R10 range mapping; R3: −0.5 average risk (1.5), the baseline (2.0), close to "+1 low risk" average (2.75) | judgment |
| Confidence | HIGH: coverage ≥ .75 and quality ≥ .75; MODERATE: ≥ .5 and ≥ .6; else LOW; −1 level with an open conflict | R11; PADL #142 | judgment |
| Confidence gate | READY/STRONG need ≥ MODERATE, else capped at DEVELOPING | R4 (claims are not readiness); R12 #105 | judgment |
| Coverage | dimension scored at ≥ 40% of graded applicable criteria; overall and group bands need ≥ 60% of stage weight scored; overall needs Team + Fundamentals scored | R1, R5, R6 (decisive factors); R12 #68 | judgment |
| Excluded from grade | Investor Fit (weight 0, routed to discovery fit); valuation and dilution recorded, ungraded; no capital-form output | R12 FSR §4, #74; R5 valuation is a price factor | locked rule |

Not found or not adopted: an OECD-specific readiness index (R7 is EC/academic); a16z's own checklist; IRL level definitions (graphic only); 2X Challenge criteria (search failed; relevant to a gender-lens module later).
