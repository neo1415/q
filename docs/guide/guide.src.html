Capital Q: a guide to every feature

Where to go, what each page does, and what to do there. With step-by-step stories for investors and founders.

Screenshots taken from the live product on using fictional demo accounts. Every company, investor and person shown is made up for demonstration.

Contents

1. What Capital Q is

Capital Q helps investors and founders of private companies find each other and decide with evidence. At its centre is Q, an AI investment analyst you can type or talk to. Investors set out what they invest in (their mandate). Capital Q then shows them companies that fit, explains why, keeps track of every relationship, and helps them reach a decision. Founders upload what they already have. Q reads it, builds their company profile and deck, shows them what investors will question, matches them with investors, and helps them practise and apply. Capital Q is not a marketplace or a chatbot. It keeps facts and opinions apart, says plainly when something is unknown, and never acts for you without your approval.

Three things to know before you start

  1. Q asks before it acts. When Q prepares something that matters, like a message, an expression of interest or a meeting, you see it first and click Approve. Nothing is sent until you do.
  2. Unknown stays unknown. If a fact is missing, Capital Q says “unknown”. It never counts a gap against a company.
  3. Private stays private. A founder’s private documents and notes never shape what an investor sees unless the founder shares them.

2. Getting in: create an account, sign in, meet Q

Capital Q runs in any modern web browser on a computer, tablet or phone. The address is https://capital-qweb-production.up.railway.app.

Creating a new account

  1. On the home page, click Get started.
  2. Type your name (what Q should call you), and your company or fund if you like. The company is optional; it helps Q find the right you.
  3. Type your email address and a password of at least 8 characters. Or click Continue with Google.
  4. Click Create account. Capital Q emails you a link to confirm your address. Open it.
  5. Q greets you and asks what brings you to Capital Q. You say whether you are a founder or an investor, and Q starts your set-up (see section 4.23).

Signing in

  1. Click Sign in on the home page.
  2. Type one of the demo email addresses below, then the shared demo password the team gives you.
  3. Click Sign in. You land on the Q page. (You can also use Google, or ask for an emailed sign-in link.)

4. Every page, one by one

Each page below says what it is for, shows what you’ll see, lists what you can do, and explains how Q helps there.

4.1 Q (your home page)

What it’s for: this is where you start each day. Q greets you, tells you what it did since your last visit, and shows what needs you. You can type or talk to Q here.

1 Talk with Q (voice) 2 type a message 3 past conversations 4 the Board, where Q pins its answers

What you can do

  1. Read the greeting and the short list of what changed this week.
  2. Click a suggestion such as Your feed or Your mandate to start there.
  3. Type a question in Message Q at the bottom and press Enter. For example: “Compare Ledgerfold and Harbourline Cargo against my mandate.”
  4. Click Talk with Q to speak instead. Allow the microphone when your browser asks. Q answers out loud and shows captions.
  5. Click Conversations to reopen an earlier chat. Click New chat to start fresh.
  6. Click Board to see the cards, tables and comparisons Q has made for you.

What Q’s answers look like

Q answers in plain words first, then adds a card or table when a picture helps. Switch between the Q view (Q’s light and what needs you) and the Chat view (the full conversation) with the buttons at the top. A Q card shows each company, what is known, what does not match, and what is still unknown. Each measure has its own label, such as Good, Partial or Unknown, so you never rely on colour alone. Q always says how many sources it used, and that a fit score is about your mandate, not the quality of the business.

How Q helps here

Q can answer questions, compare companies, open any page for you (“open the Ledgerfold record”), click buttons on the page you are on, and prepare actions such as a message or an expression of interest. Anything that changes something for another person waits for your Approve.

Limits

Voice needs a microphone and a supported browser (recent Chrome, Edge or Safari). The voice screen is not pictured in this guide because starting it opens a live, paid voice session.

4.2 Discover

What it’s for (investors): a feed of short company pitch videos, ranked against your mandate. Next to each video you see the key facts and why it’s here. Nobody can pay to be ranked higher.

1 Interest 2 Save 3 Pass 4 Ask Q about this company 5 Open the full profile 6 Filters

What you can do

  1. Watch the pitch. Use the up and down arrows (or your keyboard’s arrow keys) to move between companies. Press Space to pause.
  2. Read Why it’s here to see which parts of your mandate the company matches.
  3. Click Save to keep it for later. The company is not told.
  4. Click Pass to move it out of your feed. You can undo this from Passed.
  5. Click Interest, read the note, then click Express interest to tell the founders you’d like to explore. This is not a commitment to invest.
  6. Click Open profile to see everything about the company.
  7. Use Filters, Verified and Has pitch video to narrow the feed. Click Your companies to see the companies you already have relationships with.

What it’s for (founders): a list of investors who made themselves visible, checked against your company. The list is based only on what investors publish; their private mandates never shape it. A second tab, Founders’ videos, shows other companies’ pitches.

How Q helps here

Click Ask Q beside a video to get Q’s view of the company against your mandate. Say “save this one” or “next company” and Q does it for you.

4.3 Saved, Passed and Compare

What it’s for: the companies you saved or passed, and a side-by-side comparison of two to four of them.

  1. In Discover, click Saved (or Passed) near the top.
  2. Tick the companies you want to compare.
  3. Click Compare side by side for a table, or Compare with Q for Q’s written view.

4.4 Explore

What it’s for: browsing everything on Capital Q, not just your matches. Pitches are grouped by topic. Search sits at the top.

  1. Click a tab such as New this week or a sector.
  2. Click a pitch to watch it and open the company.
  3. Type in the search box and press Enter. Results are split into Companies, Investors, People and Pitches.

4.5 Search

What it’s for: finding a person or company by name among your relationships and Discover, or opening anyone’s Q Card by their @handle. Go to it from Explore or by typing /search after the web address.

4.6 A company profile

What it’s for: everything Capital Q knows about one company, with the evidence behind it. Investors see how it fits their mandate. Founders see what investors would see.

1 Overview 2 Elevator (pitch videos) 3 Data room 4 Pitch deck 5 Team 6 jump to your CRM record

What you can do

  1. Read Fit with your mandate. Click See why for the reasons.
  2. Scroll to Questions to investigate, Risks and unknowns and What is known, and how well supported. Each fact shows where it came from.
  3. Click a ready-made question, such as “What are the round terms?”, to ask Q.
  4. Click Send questions to send your questions to the founder.
  5. Open the Pitch deck tab to flip through the deck, or click Full screen.
  6. Open the Data room tab to open shared documents or request the ones marked “On request”.
  7. Open the Team tab to see the founders and their roles.

How Q helps here

Click Ask Q to check the evidence or any “Ask Q about …” button. Q tells you what is verified, what the company only claims, and what is unknown.

4.7 An investor profile and mandate

What it’s for (founders): what an investor looks for, checked against your company, rule by rule. It uses only their public profile and their published GateQ rules, never their private thesis.

  1. Read What this investor looks for. Each rule says Met, Not met or Unknown.
  2. Click Apply via GateQ to apply through their gate, Rehearse to practise a meeting with them, or Open in CRM if you are already connected.
  3. If you aren’t connected yet, use Connect to ask to connect.

Your own investor profile: click Profile in the You group. It shows your name, role and firm, your typical cheque, stages and key sectors, and an About section. Click Edit profile to change it, Share Q Card to share it, or Visibility to choose whether founders can find you.

Your mandate is the set of rules you invest by: stage, cheque size, places, sectors, business types, and green and red flags. It is private to your firm. You see it on the CRM Capital tab; click Review my mandate to change it, or ask Q to “sharpen my mandate”. Discover ranks companies against it.

4.8 Capital (a tab in the CRM)

What it’s for (investors): your money and your mandate in one place: what you have committed, what needs you, and the rules you invest by. Click CRM, then the Capital tab.

  1. Under Needs you, click Mark as sent once you have wired agreed money. The company then confirms it arrived.
  2. Click Review my mandate to change your stage, cheque size, sectors, places or red flags.
  3. Use the quick buttons, such as Companies that fit my mandate or Where are my deals?, to ask Q.

What it’s for (founders): your raise. The CRM Capital tab, and the full Capital page that Q links to from your home page (“Action plan”, “Readiness”), show Overview, Raise & rounds, Readiness, Action plan, 12-month plan and Investors.

How Q helps here

Founders can click Plan this raise, Make my pitch deck or What’s missing?. Investors can click Sharpen my mandate. Q drafts; you confirm.

4.9 CRM (Investment Hub and Fundraising Hub)

What it’s for: one record for every company (or investor) you are talking to, with its stage, history, notes, next step and documents. Investors see it as the Investment Hub, founders as the Fundraising Hub. There is only ever one record per company–investor pair, so Discover, GateQ and the CRM always agree.

The CRM has three tabs along the top: Overview, Pipeline and Capital (section 4.8).

Overview

Pipeline: list, table and board

  1. Click CRM in the sidebar.
  2. Click Pipeline, then List, Table or Board.
  3. Use the stage filter and sort menus. Click Save view to keep a set-up you like.
  4. Click Export to download the list.
  5. Click Preview for a quick look, or click a name to open its record.

A record

The bar across the top shows the stages: Interest, Connected, Met, Diligence, Invested. Under Deal you see Soft commit, Terms, Signed, Funds received and Closed. Any change makes a new version; nothing is overwritten.

Meetings

Open the Calls tab on a record to book a meeting, or have Q join it. Below, you see every step of the relationship with its date.

What you can do on a record

  1. Write a private note and click Save note.
  2. Click Book a call, then Find a time.
  3. Click Send a message or open the Messages tab.
  4. Click Record the terms or File a report as the deal moves on.
  5. Click Set a reminder so Q nudges you later.
  6. Record your decision with Pause for now or Not proceeding for now.
  7. Click Audit trail (CSV) to download every step with who did it and when.

How Q helps here

Click Let Q handle this and Q takes care of the follow-up, such as replying or finding a meeting time, asking for your yes before anything is sent. Click Ask Q about this relationship for a summary of where things stand.

Limits

Booking a call needs your Google Calendar connected in Settings → Connections. Without it, Capital Q tells you so and you can’t pick a time yet. Having Q join a live call has not yet been proven in a live meeting.

4.10 Company requests and investor interest

Company requests (investors): companies that asked to connect with you, ranked by fit. Accepting connects you; it is not an investment.

Investor interest (founders): investors who expressed interest in your company. Click Accept to connect, or Decline.

4.11 GateQ

What it’s for: GateQ is an investor’s front door. The investor publishes a few simple rules (stage, place, sector, cheque size). Founders check their fit in two minutes and apply. Q interviews them and turns the answers into a short dossier for the investor.

Investors: the inbox and the dossier

1 Inbox 2 Find a startup 3 Your gate 4 Triage 5 Reply 6 Pass

  1. Click GateQ, then Inbox.
  2. Use the views on the left: Starred, Fits, Partial, Not a fit, Passed, Archived.
  3. Click an application to open its dossier.
  4. Read the brief, the rules it meets, and anything needing clarification.
  5. Click Reply to answer, Pass to decline politely, Download pack to save it, or Add note for your team.

Investors: your gate

  1. Choose who can apply: Closed, Only companies that meet my rules, or Anyone.
  2. Click Edit to change your rules, then Save.
  3. Share the gate with Copy link, a QR code, or a Website snippet for your own site.

Founders: applying

The interview is video first. After the short form, Q offers a short video interview: Q appears on screen and asks a few questions about your company. You agree to the recording, then start it. If video isn’t offered or you would rather type, you answer the same questions in writing. When you finish, you review the summary and press Submit. The investor gets a dossier with your answers in your own words, and Q’s reading of them, clearly labelled.

How Q helps here

Investors can ask Q to triage the inbox, summarise an application, draft a reply or a pass, label or star applications. Founders get a short interview from Q instead of a long form, and can ask Q “do we fit?” before applying.

Limits

The video interview uses a paid video service with a monthly allowance, so it appears only when it is switched on and there is time left. Typing is always available.

4.12 Rehearsals

What it’s for: practising a meeting before the real one. Q plays the other side by voice (an investor for founders, a founder for investors), based only on public and shared information. Afterwards you get a scored review.

  1. Click Rehearsals, then Rehearse next to a name.
  2. Read the lobby notes. Pick Q’s voice, how hard they should be (Gentle, Realistic or Tough), and how you’ll meet (Voice room, or Video for up to 3 minutes when available).
  3. Click Join now and allow your microphone.
  4. Talk as you would in the real meeting. End when you are ready.
  5. Read the review and click Rehearse again to try once more.

Limits

Your plan sets how many rehearsals you get each month; the page tells you how many are left.

4.13 Q Work

What it’s for: the jobs you give Q, and everything Q needs your yes for. Sections: Needs you, Q suggests, Done for you and In progress.

  1. Click Give Q a task and describe the job in a sentence.
  2. Watch it appear under In progress.
  3. When Q needs you, it appears under Needs you. Read the exact message or action, then approve or change it.
  4. Under Done for you, click Thread to see what was sent and the replies.
  5. Click a suggestion under Q suggests, such as “Kazikit hasn’t replied. Follow up?”, to start it.

Q’s team at work

Behind the one Q you talk to, a team of specialists does the steps of a bigger job: research, documents, writing, reviewing, scheduling, outreach. Click Team map to see who is working, who is idle and what each one did. You never pick or talk to them directly.

Limits

Cost shows what Q’s work has cost so far. You can pause Q’s work at any time.

4.14 Documents, data room and deck

What it’s for: every file you have uploaded and every document Q has written for you, such as investment memos and briefs. Each file says who can see it.

  1. Click Upload and pick your files. Q reads each one.
  2. Use the filters (Decks, Financials, Legal, By Q, Shared) or search.
  3. Click a document’s actions menu to rename, share with an investor, or archive it.
  4. Open Requested to see documents investors have asked for.

Data room (founders)

Click Data room & deck in the sidebar. Documents sit in standard folders. For each document you choose Public, On request, Shared only or Private. Investors see the data room once you accept their interest. Capital Q shows how many of the usual documents for your round you already have.

When an investor asks for a document, it shows under Waiting for you. Click Share to send it for a set time (for example 30 days), or Not now.

Deck (founders)

When you upload a deck, Q reads it section by section. Investors see a section only after you confirm Q read it correctly.

4.15 Pitch & media (founders)

What it’s for: your pitch videos. A live video appears in investors’ Discover feeds, subject to who you let see it.

  1. Click Pitch & media, then New video.
  2. Upload your video and give it a title.
  3. Choose who can see it, then publish.

4.16 The Q Daily

What it’s for: a short news edition written for you, following your sectors and stage. Each story links to its source.

  1. Click The Q Daily.
  2. Read the lead story, then scroll for your sector, deals and Q’s take.
  3. Click Download PDF to keep it, or Change how often to pick daily or weekly.

4.17 Q Pitch

What it’s for: a guided tour of Capital Q that Q leads by voice, through the real app and real (demo) data. The Investor premiere is one continuous tour of about 5 to 7 minutes. The Two-minute version is a short taster. The Feature Atlas lets you open one chapter at a time when you want to go deeper.

  1. Click Q Pitch in the Workspace group. Turn your sound on.
  2. Click Start beside Investor premiere or Two-minute version. Q takes you to the Q page; click Start the tour to hear it, or Captions only.
  3. Q moves through the app on its own: your mandate, your feed and top three, saving a company, one company on the evidence, risks, a side-by-side comparison, what Q knows versus what you may see, GateQ and an applicant’s transcript, the CRM record and its history, preparing a meeting, Q Work, Documents and The Q Daily.
  4. Use the on-screen controls at any time: Back, Pause, Show me that again, Skip, Chapters and End. Press Escape to pause, or just ask a question out loud.
  5. To see one topic only, scroll to the Feature Atlas and click Open beside a chapter.

Limits

Q Pitch is for investor workspaces. Founders see a note that a founder edition hasn’t been built yet. In the premiere, 11 of 17 scenes have been checked in a live browser; the rest are marked internal.

4.18 Profile and Q Card

What it’s for: what other people see about you and your company. Edit any section in place. Your Q Card is a short, shareable summary of your company with a link and QR code that works outside Capital Q.

4.19 Settings, plan and usage

What it’s for: your account, team, appearance, how Q speaks, notifications, connections (such as Google), plan and billing, and privacy.

  • Appearance: light, device or dark; how much Q’s light moves; sounds.
  • Q: Q’s voice (female or male) and personality (warm, witty, sharp).
  • How Q speaks for you: write a guide for the tone Q uses in your name.
  • Notifications and The Q Daily frequency.
  • Connections: connect Google to book calls.

Coming soon

Language. Capital Q is in English today. Arabic and other languages, with a language choice in Settings, are being built.

Limits

Everyone is on the free Launch plan while Capital Q launches. To change plan, click Ask to change plan.

4.20 Team and invitations

What it’s for: the people who work on your company or firm with you, and what each role can do. Owner: everything, plus billing and handing over ownership. Admin: invites people, edits the profile, approves what Q sends. Member: reviews, writes notes, works the inbox; can suggest but not approve.

4.21 Notifications and approvals

What it’s for: one list of what happened and what needs your yes. Click the bell at the bottom of the sidebar.

  1. Click the bell.
  2. Click an item to open it.
  3. For “needs your yes” items, click Open Q’s work to approve or change them.
  4. Click Turn on notifications to get alerts on this device.

4.22 Verification

What it’s for: a person at Capital Q checks your identity and your organisation’s registered details. Verified means checked; it is not an endorsement.

4.23 Getting started (onboarding)

Founders: “upload what you already have, Q works first, then fill gaps.” The steps are: what brings you here, your company, stage, what the company does, how you would categorise it, what you already have (pitch deck, financial model, management accounts, company memo), then “Here’s what we have so far”, your founding team, business and traction, and whether you are raising now. You can skip any step; unknown is fine. Q shows you useful results before it asks for a video or verification.

Investors: capture your mandate quickly: which mandate, whether you are deploying now, stage and cheque, where you invest, sectors, business attributes, green and red flags, then “Your mandate is ready”. Your personalised feed appears straight away.

Q runs the set-up as a conversation. You can talk or type, skip any question, and come back later. Q fills in what it can from your documents and website and asks you to confirm anything important before it counts.

Coming soon

A new, more intelligent onboarding is being built: Q will read more of what you already have and ask even fewer questions. This guide will be refreshed when it ships.

Limits

The demo accounts have already finished onboarding, so this guide doesn’t show the first-time screens. To see them, click Create account on the sign-in page and use your own email address.

4.24 On a phone or tablet

Everything works on a phone. The sidebar becomes a bar at the bottom with Discover, Explore, Q, CRM and More. Tap More for GateQ, Work, Documents, Rehearsals, Settings and the rest. The bell and the theme switch sit at the top. Pages stack into one column; lists open their details on a new screen.

On a tablet held upright (about 834 pixels wide), Capital Q uses the phone layout with more room: the bottom bar replaces the sidebar and wider pages such as the pipeline board keep their columns.

5. Step-by-step stories

Each story follows one person through a real task, from start to finish, using the demo accounts.

Story 1. An investor finds a company and decides

Sign in as Amara (investor). Time: about 15 minutes.

  1. Open Discover. Click Discover. You see Ledgerfold, a seed fintech in Lagos. Read Why it’s here: stage, place and sector all match your mandate.
  2. Save it. Click Save. It goes to your Saved list. Ledgerfold is not told.
  3. Ask Q. Click Ask Q, or open Q and ask “What are the main risks with Ledgerfold?”. Q answers with a card that separates what is verified from what is claimed or unknown.
  4. Compare. Click Saved, tick Ledgerfold and another company, and click Compare side by side. Or ask Q: “Compare Ledgerfold and Harbourline Cargo against my mandate.”
  5. Read the full profile. Click Open profile. Check the deck, the data room and the team tabs.
  6. Express interest. Back in Discover, click Interest, then Express interest. Ledgerfold’s founders are told you would like to explore. In this demo, Ledgerfold has already accepted, so you are Connected.
  7. Follow up in the CRM. Click CRM. Ledgerfold is in your pipeline. Click its name to open the record.
  8. Write a private note. Scroll to Private notes, type your thoughts, and click Save note. Only your team sees it.
  9. Book a meeting. Click Book a call, then Find a time. (Connect Google Calendar in Settings first.) You can also click Have Q join a call.
  10. Record your decision. After the meeting, move the deal forward with Record the terms, or record Pause for now or Not proceeding for now. If you go ahead, the commitment appears on the CRM Capital tab, where you click Mark as sent once the money is wired.

Story 2. A founder gets ready, applies and tracks interest

Sign in as Babatunde (Harbourline Cargo). Ledgerfold’s account shows the same steps further along.

  1. Onboard. A new founder answers a few short questions and uploads what they already have. Q reads it and fills in the profile. (See section 4.23.)
  2. Upload documents. Click Documents, then Upload. Add your deck, accounts and team summary. Each shows “Q has read it”.
  3. Check what Q read in your deck. Open the Pitch deck tab on your company. Mark each section Looks right or This is wrong, then click Confirm all and show.
  4. Let Q build the deck. On Capital, click Make my pitch deck. Q drafts an investor deck from your documents. Ledgerfold’s founder already has one.
  5. Set up the data room. Click Data room & deck. Choose who can see each document.
  6. Publish a pitch. Click Pitch & media, then New video, upload, choose who can see it, and publish. Your pitch now appears in investors’ Discover feeds.
  7. Find an investor. Click Discover and open Savanna Seed Partners. Check which of their published rules you meet.
  8. Apply through GateQ. Open the investor’s gate. Confirm your details and check the four short steps Q filled in. Q then offers a short video interview first; agree to the recording and start it, or answer the same questions by typing. Review, then press Submit.
  9. Rehearse. Click Rehearsals, then Rehearse beside the investor. Q plays the investor by voice. Read your review afterwards.
  10. Track interest in the CRM. When an investor expresses interest, it appears on Q and under Investor interest. Click Accept to connect. Then follow each investor in your Fundraising Hub.

Story 3. An investor reviews a GateQ application and decides

Sign in as Amara.

  1. Click GateQ. The inbox opens on Inbox. Applications show how many of your rules they meet.
  2. Click Fits to see only the applications that meet your rules. Click one, for example Maji Loop.
  3. Read the dossier: the submitted investment brief, what they shared, any rules needing clarification, and what has happened so far.
  4. Ask Q: “Summarise this application and tell me what to ask.” Q answers from the dossier only.
  5. Add a note for your team with Add note, or star it.
  6. Decide. Click Reply to invite them to a call, or Pass. Q can draft the pass for you; you approve the exact words before it is sent. Either way, the founder hears back, as your gate promises.

Story 4. A team owner invites a teammate

Sign in as Babatunde (owner of Harbourline Cargo).

  1. Click You in the sidebar, then Settings, then Team.
  2. Click Invite people. Type the person’s email (you can add several, separated by commas), choose Member or Admin, add a short message if you like, and click Send invitation.
  3. The teammate gets an email. When they sign in to Capital Q with that email, they see an invitation card. They click Accept and join the company. They can also decline.
  4. Back on the Team page, the new person appears with their role. You can change their role, resend or cancel an invitation, or remove someone.

Not pictured

The acceptance screen is not shown: taking it would send a real invitation from a demo account.

Story 5. Q Work: a multi-step diligence job, approved by you

Sign in as Amara. This job already exists on the demo account, so you can follow it without starting a new one.

  1. Click Work. In Give Q a task, Amara typed: “Get the team ready for our Harbourline Cargo diligence: research, a reviewed memo and founder questions.”
  2. Q showed its plan and its budget, and waited. Amara clicked Approve. Nothing starts until you approve the plan.
  3. Q’s team did four steps: research on the company, a document review, an investment brief that a second specialist checked, and five questions for the founder. Click Team map to see who did what.
  4. The job shows under Done for you. Click Open document to read the investment brief Q wrote.
  5. The five founder questions go to another company, so Q did not send them. They wait under Needs you, word for word.
  6. Read them. Click Approve to send them exactly as written, or Decline. If you ask Q to change the wording, you approve the new words again.

Good to know

Some older test jobs on the demo account show “Failed” or “Not allowed to do this step”, each with a plain reason; nothing was sent for those. The Cost figure at the top of Q Work shows what Q’s work has cost so far.

Story 6. Running Q Pitch

Sign in as Amara. Turn your sound on. The premiere takes 5 to 7 minutes.

  1. Click Q Pitch in the Workspace group.
  2. Click Start beside Investor premiere. Q opens the Q page and asks how to present; click Start the tour (or Captions only). Q introduces itself and begins.
  3. Sit back. Q opens each page itself and explains what you see, from your mandate to the CRM and Q Work.
  4. Interrupt whenever you like: click Pause or Back, say “show me GateQ”, or ask a question. Q answers, then carries on.
  5. Click End when you are done. Later, open a single chapter from the Feature Atlas.

6. Glossary

Approve
Your yes to an action Q prepared. It covers exactly what you saw; if anything changes, Q asks again.
Board
The panel on the Q page where Q keeps its cards, tables and comparisons.
Commitment
An amount an investor says they will invest, confirmed by the company. It moves from agreed, to sent, to received.
Connected
Both sides agreed to talk. It is not an investment.
CRM
Where you track every relationship. Investors call it the Investment Hub; founders the Fundraising Hub.
Data room
A founder’s folders of company documents. The founder decides, document by document, who can see what.
Discover
The ranked video feed of companies (for investors) or the list of investors (for founders).
Dossier
The short report Q makes from a GateQ application, for the investor to review.
Express interest
Telling a company you would like to explore. It is not a commitment.
GateQ
An investor’s front door: published rules, a two-minute fit check for founders, a short interview by Q, and an inbox of dossiers.
InvestIQ
Capital Q’s method for judging how ready a company is to raise. Founders see it as Readiness on the Capital page: each area in words, with its evidence and gaps. It does not give a single score yet.
Mandate
What an investor invests in: stage, cheque size, places, sectors, business types, and red flags. Discover ranks companies against it.
Mandate fit
How well a company matches your mandate. It is not a judgement of the business.
Pass
Moving a company out of your feed, or declining an application. You can undo a pass in Discover.
Pipeline
Your relationships by stage, shown as a list, table or board.
Q
Capital Q’s AI investment analyst. You talk to one Q, wherever you are in the app.
Q Card
A short, shareable summary of a company, with a link and QR code, that works outside Capital Q.
Q Daily
Your personal news edition, daily or weekly.
Q Pitch
A guided tour of Capital Q, narrated by Q.
Q Work
Jobs you have given Q, and the steps waiting for your approval.
Readiness
See InvestIQ.
Rehearsal
A practice meeting where Q plays the other side by voice, followed by a scored review.
Save
Keeping a company for later. The company is not told, and saving is not interest.
Unknown
A fact Capital Q doesn’t have. It never counts against anyone.
Verified
Checked by a person at Capital Q. Not an endorsement.
Visibility
Who can see something: just you, your team, a specific investor, everyone on Capital Q, or the public web.

7. Limits and what’s coming

Works today, with limits

  • Booking calls needs Google Calendar connected in Settings → Connections.
  • Video interviews in GateQ run on a paid video service with a monthly allowance. When it is off or used up, founders type their answers instead.
  • Rehearsals, Q Daily, web research and other Q features have monthly allowances on the free Launch plan. See Settings → Plan.
  • Voice needs a microphone and a recent Chrome, Edge or Safari.
  • Pitch videos need a browser that plays standard web video. Very locked-down browsers show “This pitch can’t play here”; the rest of the profile still works.
  • Readiness is described in words. A single InvestIQ score will come only once the scoring method is formally approved.
  • Red-flag rules in a mandate can’t all be checked automatically yet. When one can’t, nobody is hidden because of it, and Discover says so.

Coming soon

  • Arabic and other languages, chosen in Settings.
  • A new, more intelligent onboarding that asks even fewer questions.
  • Q Pitch for founders. Today the tour is for investors only.
  • Q joining live meetings. “Have Q join a call” is built but has not yet been proven in a live meeting.
  • More Q Pitch chapters, including meetings, the company data room, readiness and the founder journey.
  • Switching between a founder and an investor workspace with one sign-in.
  • Guest document uploads in GateQ applications.
  • Ongoing polish. Small layout fixes ship often, so a screen may look slightly different from these pictures.
